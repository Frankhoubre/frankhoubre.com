Pick Higgsfield if your job is making shots from nothing: around twenty video models under one subscription, plus Cinema Studio 4.0 to set camera, light and references before you hit generate. Pick Runway if you already have footage and want to fix it: Aleph 2.0 changes one element in a clip and leaves the rest alone, on up to 30 seconds at 1080p, and the Max plan exports ProRes and HDR. Run the same model on both and a 5-second shot lands within a few cents. So the real decision is whether you mostly generate or mostly correct.

I co-founded ScreenWeaver, which runs several of the models these two sell. This article also has an affiliate link to Higgsfield and none to Runway, because I never joined Runway's program. So I stick to what both companies publish, checked on October 25, 2026, and I didn't run a single generation for this comparison. When something is my opinion, I say so.

## Two different ideas of AI video

Put the two pricing pages next to each other. Higgsfield sells you models: its cost grid lists Seedance from 1.5 to 2.0, Kling from 2.1 to 3.0, Veo 3 and 3.1, Wan from 2.2 to 3.0, Hailuo, Grok Video and its own DoP models. On top of that it stacks studios (Cinema Studio, Marketing Studio, Ads Studio, AI Influencer) and a catalog of one-click effects.

Runway comes from research. It trains its own models, Gen-4.5 to generate and Aleph 2.0 to edit, and adds other people's models next to them: Seedance 2.5, Kling 3.0, Nano Banana Pro. The changelog tells the same story. In one month Runway shipped a DaVinci Resolve plugin on September 23, ElevenLabs V4 on September 29, then a run of audio and background removal tools in its MCP server in mid October. Runway keeps moving toward post-production, while Higgsfield keeps moving toward shot-making.

My take: that split matters more than any model leaderboard. An aggregator gets you the newest engine fastest. A company with its own models gets you features nobody else has, like Aleph's edits. Nobody sells you both in one box.

## Making a shot: Cinema Studio vs Gen-4.5

On Higgsfield, the tool built for directors is Cinema Studio, now at version 4.0. It opens without an account. Before you even type a prompt you get five controls: up to 50 references, film setup, camera, color palette and lighting. The text field tells you to type @ to call a character or location you already built.

You pay for those camera controls. On Runway, the front door is now an Agent. The app opens in guest mode on one question, "What will you create today?", with a slot for a reference or a sketch and shortcuts to the timeline, Seedance 2.5 and MCP. The left menu leads to the classic tool, Apps, Workflows, plugins and Brand Kits.

To generate a shot, Gen-4.5 costs 60 credits per 5 seconds, or 12 credits a second according to the pricing FAQ. At launch [in December 2025](https://runway.com/research/introducing-runway-gen-4.5), Runway called it number one on a text-to-video leaderboard. That's Runway's claim, and the rankings have moved since. What still holds: you'll only find Gen-4.5 on Runway.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** before choosing, look at your last three projects. If most shots started from an image or a prompt, you need models and camera controls. If you spent your evenings fixing clips that were almost right, you need an editing tool.

## Fixing existing footage: where Aleph 2.0 pulls ahead

Aleph 2.0 does one specific thing. You change one frame of your video, or describe the change in words, and the model carries it through the whole clip while keeping everything else intact. The official page gives examples: change a product's color, add graffiti to a wall, remove objects, turn the scene to winter, tighten the frame on a product.

<Screenshot src="runway-aleph-2-2026-10-25.webp" alt="Runway Aleph 2.0 page headed A new way to modify video, with four features: edit one frame to update the whole video, change a specific element, edit across multiple shots, up to 30 seconds" caption="Aleph 2.0's four promises on October 25. The last one is the one an editor cares about: 30 seconds at 1080p, with multiple shots and cuts." source="https://runway.com/product/aleph-2" capturedAt="2026-10-25" />

Aleph handles clips with several cuts and can apply your edit to the shots it concerns. Edit Studio also previews the change as a still before you generate, so you don't pay just to see what happens. At 140 credits per 5 seconds, that preview earns its place.

Higgsfield has no documented equivalent. It offers targeted edits as apps (face swap, character swap, outfit swap, relighting, image expansion) and a Grok Video Edit model in its grid. There is an "Edit" entry in its navigation, but none of the pages I read that day describe long-clip editing comparable to Aleph. If you want to keep a clip and change one detail, Runway is ahead.

Finishing points the same way. Runway's Max plan lists HDR, ProRes and image sequence output, the formats a colorist or editor will ask for. Higgsfield's pricing page documents nothing like it.

## Refunds, credits and reputation

On credits, Runway is more flexible. Extra credits you buy never expire, and Max rolls over one month of unused credits. On Higgsfield, the official help center says unused subscription credits expire at each renewal (every 30 days on annual plans), and packs and auto-refills expire 90 days after purchase.

The written refund rules differ too. Higgsfield refunds a first purchase within 7 days, only if no credits were used, with a fee of up to 6%, and never refunds a renewal, per [its help page](https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/refunds/how-do-i-request-a-refund). Runway will consider a request within 30 days of a purchase or renewal if the plan went unused "or close to unused", at its discretion, per [its help center](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/24343363554067-Requesting-a-refund-for-your-plan-or-payment). On both, a bad generation keeps its credits. Runway only gives them back when the generation ends in an error.

## What both are missing for a full film

Neither Higgsfield nor Runway starts from your script. Neither documents a proper screenplay editor, or a shot breakdown that stays tied to the text when you rewrite a scene. Runway has node-based Workflows and an Agent, Higgsfield has characters and locations you call with @ in Cinema Studio. Good shot tools, both of them. A twenty-minute short is a different job: continuity, characters that must stay the same across dozens of shots, and a script that keeps changing.

## Higgsfield or Runway: which one for your project

Go with Higgsfield if you generate lots of short shots from images or prompts, want to switch models without switching subscriptions, and make ads, social content or staging tests. Go straight to Plus: Starter doesn't give you every Seedance or every model. And read the unlimited terms before you rely on them.

Go with Runway if you work on footage that already exists, yours or a client's, and want to fix it without regenerating it. It's also the pick if you want a model you can't get anywhere else, if you cut in DaVinci Resolve, or if you have to deliver ProRes or HDR, in which case you need Max.

Nothing stops you from using both. Generating shots in an aggregator and polishing the best ones in Runway is a real workflow. For keeping characters consistent across shots, whatever the tool, read [how to keep the same character from shot to shot](/en/blog/garder-meme-personnage-video-ia).

## FAQ

### Which is cheaper, Higgsfield or Runway?

Whichever runs your favorite model for less. On annual billing, Runway starts at $12 a month for 625 credits and Higgsfield at €19 before tax for 270 credits. The credit price alone doesn't settle it. A 5-second Seedance 2.0 shot at 1080p works out to about €2.35 before tax on Higgsfield Plus and $2.49 on Runway Pro. Kling 3.0 on Higgsfield drops to around 30 cents, and Gen-4.5 on Runway to 75 cents. Check what your favorite model costs on each platform before you compare subscriptions.

### Can you use Higgsfield and Runway for free?

Yes, with limits. Runway gives you 125 one-time credits that never expire and access to a selection of models, which is about 10 seconds of Gen-4.5. Higgsfield has a Free plan described as "limited use", with one job at a time and no commercial use. Neither free tier is enough to judge a tool on a real project. They're for learning the interface. For a proper test, budget at least one month on a paid plan, and read the refund rules before you subscribe.

### Which is better for editing existing video?

Runway, with Aleph 2.0. The model changes one specific element in a clip, like a color, a background or an object to remove, and keeps the rest intact. It handles clips up to 30 seconds at 1080p, including ones with several cuts, and Edit Studio previews the change as a still before you generate. Budget 140 credits per 5 seconds. Higgsfield offers edits as apps (face, character, outfit, relighting) and a Grok Video Edit model, but nothing comparable is documented for a long clip.

### Which is better for generating cinematic shots?

For range of engines and staging controls, Higgsfield. Cinema Studio 4.0 lets you set camera, palette, lighting and up to 50 references before generating, and the catalog holds around twenty video models. Runway answers with Gen-4.5, its in-house model, cheaper per shot, plus third-party models like Seedance 2.5 and Kling 3.0. If you want to compare several engines on the same shot, the aggregator is more practical. If you want output you can't get elsewhere, Gen-4.5 is only on Runway.

### Can I use the videos for a client?

On Higgsfield, commercial use is ticked from Starter up and crossed out on Free, in the detailed comparison on the pricing page. On Runway, the usage rights help page says the content you generate is yours, with no non-commercial restrictions from Runway. That page doesn't split rights by plan. Only the Standard plan explicitly mentions no watermarks. Either way, you're responsible for what you upload: a face, a brand or a song you don't have the rights to doesn't become free because an AI processed it.

### Which one makes refunds easier?

On paper, Runway gives you more room: 30 days after a purchase or renewal, if the plan went unused or close to it, at Runway's discretion. Higgsfield limits refunds to first purchases, within 7 days, with no credits used, a fee of up to 6%, and never refunds a renewal. On both, a disappointing generation uses up its credits. Runway only returns them when a generation fails with a technical error. If you only want to test, turn off auto-renewal right after subscribing.

### Can you make a full short film with either one?

Technically yes, but neither is built for it. Neither starts from a script, and neither keeps the link between a written scene and its generated shots when the text changes. You'll have to handle continuity, character references and the shot breakdown somewhere else, in a spreadsheet or another tool. For a short project, an ad or a music video, that's workable. For a scripted short or a series, a tool that starts from the screenplay will keep you from losing the thread, and you can still polish the best shots in Runway afterward.
