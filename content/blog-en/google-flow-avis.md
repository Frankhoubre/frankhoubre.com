Yes, you can make a short film in Google Flow, as long as you treat it as a string of 8 to 10 second shots that you put together yourself. Flow is one of the cheapest places to start: 50 free credits a day, then Google AI Plus at $4.99 a month. Veo 3.1 and Gemini Omni run well in it, and Scenebuilder lines your shots up. What it doesn't do is connect your script to your shots, and in Europe two features are switched off. For a pitch or a teaser, it's a great place to start. For a written 10 minute film, you'll need other tools around it.

I co-founded ScreenWeaver, which sells a script, storyboard and production pipeline, and which also runs Veo 3.1 among its models. So Flow is a competitor. I didn't run a single generation in Flow for this review. Everything below comes from Google's official pages checked on October 27, 2026, the help center, a public thread on Google's forum, and math done from the official credit table. When it's my opinion, I say so.

## What Google Flow is in October 2026

Google calls Flow an AI creative studio built with its advanced generative models. It launched at Google I/O 2025 for filmmakers, then got relaunched at I/O on May 19, 2026 with an agent, custom tools, the Gemini Omni model and an Android beta app. The address moved too: the old labs.google page now redirects to [flow.google.com](https://flow.google.com/).

<Screenshot src="google-flow-accueil-2026-10-27.webp" alt="Public Google Flow home page with the Google Flow title over a mosaic of generated clips, a Create with Google Flow button and a menu with Overview, Models, Capabilities, Tools, Flow Sessions and Pricing" caption="Flow's public home page on October 27 (French locale), visible without an account. At the bottom, Google notes that features vary by subscription, platform and region, and that you must be 18 or older." source="https://flow.google.com/" capturedAt="2026-10-27" />

Three model families run inside it, all from Google: Veo 3.1 (Lite, Fast, Quality) for video with sound, Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 for video and editing, and Nano Banana (Pro, 2.1, 2 Lite) for start frames and references. No Kling, no Seedance, no MiniMax. If a shot comes out better somewhere else, you have to leave Flow to get it.

The help center also recommends writing prompts in English for the best results.

## What a director can actually do in Flow

You can start a shot four ways: from text, from a first frame, from a first and last frame, or from "ingredients" (Google also calls them references), meaning images of your character, set or prop that you want to see again in the shot. That last one matters most to a director, because it's what helps keep a face consistent from shot to shot.

Except references don't work on every model, and the official table shows it.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-modeles-2026-10-27.webp" alt="Google Flow help page in French listing the features Veo 3.1 Quality does not support: ingredients or references to video, video editing, extending videos, followed by Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 features with durations from 4 to 10 seconds" caption="Veo 3.1 Quality, the most expensive model, takes no references and can't be extended. Gemini Omni Flash 1.1 accepts references on 4 to 10 second shots." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16352836?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-27" />

Veo 3.1 Lite and Fast take references, but only on 8 second shots. Veo 3.1 Quality doesn't take them at all. Gemini Omni Flash takes them at every length, up to 10 seconds. To extend a shot you need an 8 second Veo clip and you have to use Veo 3.1 Lite, whatever model made the original. Extension for Omni is listed as coming soon.

My take: if your film has a recurring character, Omni is the first model to try in Flow, since it's the only one that combines references with 10 seconds. Save Veo 3.1 Quality for shots with no character to hold, like scenery, an insert or an establishing shot. For the consistency method itself (character sheet, angles, light), I wrote a separate guide on [keeping the same character from shot to shot](/en/blog/garder-meme-personnage-video-ia).

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** before you launch a shot with your character, check which model is active in the prompt box settings. The cost of the shot shows there too, and Google warns that its rates can change at any time, so that's where you read today's price.

## Scenebuilder, Omni and the agent: editing inside Flow

Once your shots exist, Scenebuilder puts them end to end. The help page lists exactly what it does: arrange several clips into a sequence, reorder them, trim the head and tail with handles, preview the whole sequence, download the scene.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-scenebuilder-2026-10-27.webp" alt="Google Flow help page, Build your scene section: Scenebuilder arranges several clips into a sequence, reorders them, trims the start and end with handles, previews and downloads the scene" caption="Scenebuilder's five moves according to the help center. Nothing about sound, transitions or color." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16935718?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-27" />

That's enough for a rough cut. What's missing is everything an editor does next: mixing sound from different shots, grading so two shots match, laying in music. For that, you download and open a real editor. The free version of DaVinci Resolve does the job well.

Since May, Gemini Omni Flash can also edit an existing shot. You pick a shot, select a segment of up to 10 seconds, and write what you want changed: the light, an object, on-screen text. You get up to three rounds of conversation without losing context, and the history panel keeps every version.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-edition-omni-2026-10-27.webp" alt="Google Flow help page describing a video upload of up to 60 seconds and 1 GB in mov, mp4, avi or wmv, trimmed to 30 seconds, then editing a 10 second segment with up to three rounds of conversation" caption="The limits of Omni editing: 60 s and 1 GB uploads, trimmed to 30 s, 10 s edited segments, three rounds of conversation." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16935718?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-27" />

For a director, it feels like ordering a small VFX fix in one sentence. A lighting mismatch between two shots can be fixed without regenerating everything. Each edit costs 40 credits, whatever the length.

The agent has been open to every Flow user since May. According to Google's announcement, it can act as a sounding board for a scene's dialogue, suggest plot ideas, generate several variations at once, apply an edit to a whole batch and sort your files into collections. I haven't used it, so I'm sticking to what Google says.

## Storyboard Studio and Tools: what exists for prep

The home page has a Tools section listing small programs built in natural language. Everyone can use them, and subscribers can create or remix them. Google built several itself, and one of them speaks directly to directors.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-outils-2026-10-27.webp" alt="Tools section of the Google Flow home page with Video Resizer, Image Editor, Storyboard Studio described as write a script, create the characters and visualize a storyboard, Shader Effects and Mockup" caption="Storyboard Studio, a tool built by Google, promises in one line to write a script, create the characters and visualize a storyboard. I haven't opened it." source="https://flow.google.com/" capturedAt="2026-10-27" />

Storyboard Studio gets a single line: write a script, create the characters and visualize a storyboard. It's the only trace of writing I found in Flow. The help center has no page on it, and doesn't say whether the storyboard stays tied to the text when you rewrite, or whether it produces a shot list with framing and durations. The rest of the list is smaller stuff: resize a video, apply filters, add animated text, pull frames out of an image grid, build a 360 degree environment.

My take: Flow has a foot in pre-production now, but without the link that matters when you rewrite. If scene 12 changes, nothing in the public docs says what happens to the shots you already made. For a two minute pitch, no big deal. For a film that goes through five drafts, that's where you'll lose the most time.

Nobody shoots with one take per shot. Say you make a 3 minute short, and assume (my assumption) four generations for every shot you keep. That's about 1,080 credits in Omni 720p, 1,800 in Veo 3.1 Fast, and 9,000 in Veo 3.1 Quality. A Pro subscriber who opens Flow every day gets roughly 2,500 credits a month (1,000 plus 50 a day). So a short in Omni or Fast fits in a month. In Quality, at that pace, you're looking at Ultra.

Credits don't roll over: the daily 50 vanish if you don't spend them, and so do the monthly ones. Extra AI credits you buy can expire, depending on the terms shown at purchase. To compare with a full AI film budget, my breakdown of [what an AI short film costs in 2026](/en/blog/combien-coute-court-metrage-ia-2026) covers the other line items.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** set Flow to a single output per request while you're still finding the shot. Two outputs means two billed generations, and on Veo 3.1 Quality that's 200 credits for one 8 second shot.

## What changes if you work from Europe

Flow is available in France and the whole EU for anyone over 18. Two features are switched off there, though, and Google says so plainly.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-restrictions-europe-2026-10-27.webp" alt="Google Flow help page in English: editing uploaded videos and the Avatars feature are unavailable in the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom" caption="The two European restrictions: no editing of videos you upload, no avatars." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353544?hl=en" capturedAt="2026-10-27" />

Editing uploaded videos is unavailable in the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom. So you can't take footage from your own camera and fix it with Omni from Paris, Zurich or London. Avatars are off in the same places. In every country, Flow also refuses to generate videos of prominent people and to edit uploaded videos of identifiable minors.

My take: for a European director, Flow's newest feature doesn't touch your real footage. The editing page also warns that editing is only offered in some countries, without naming them beyond that list. If your project mixes live action and AI shots, plan to do the fixes elsewhere.

## Can you use Flow videos commercially?

The help center is careful here. Google's terms of service govern how the tools may be used, and Google says it won't claim ownership of original content you generate.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-usage-commercial-2026-10-27.webp" alt="Google Flow help FAQ opened on the question can I use the outputs of these tools for commercial purposes, with an answer pointing to the terms of service and saying Google won't claim ownership of the content" caption="The only official answer on commercial use: a pointer to the terms of service, with no yes or no." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353333?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-27" />

So there's no explicit yes, and no difference shown between free and paid. Every video and image made with Veo, Omni or Nano Banana carries an invisible SynthID watermark, and a visible watermark can be switched on or off in your profile menu. For an ad or a film delivered to a client, read Google's terms in full, or go through Google Cloud's enterprise offer if your client needs a contract. I'm not a lawyer, and this page doesn't replace one.

## When to pick Google Flow, and when to pick something else

Pick Flow if you're starting out and want to learn to prompt a shot without reaching for your card, if you're cutting a teaser or an ad under a minute, or if you already work in Google's services (Gemini, Drive, YouTube). Pro also gives you 5 TB of storage, which adds up fast with video files.

Pick something else if you need Kling or Seedance for a specific kind of shot, if you're fixing live action footage from Europe, or if your project is a series where the script keeps changing. For a single carefully crafted shot, my [director's brief for Veo 3](/en/blog/google-veo-3-brief-realisateur-plan-sequence) will help you get the model right before you worry about the tool.

## FAQ

### Is Google Flow free?

Partly. The home page and the credits help page give everyone 50 Google Flow credits a day, subscribed or not. That covers five Veo 3.1 Lite shots of 8 seconds, two Veo 3.1 Fast shots, or three 10 second Gemini Omni shots in 720p. A Veo 3.1 Quality shot costs 100 credits, so it's out of reach without a plan. Daily credits don't roll over. Without a subscription, Google also warns that video may be unavailable at peak hours, roughly 2 pm to 5 pm UTC. An older help page still says a subscription is required.

### How much does Google Flow cost?

On October 27, 2026, flow.google.com lists Google AI Plus at $4.99 a month, Pro at $19.99, and Ultra at $99.99 or $199.99, with a note that prices vary by market. In France, Google's subscription page shows 4.99 €, 21.99 €, 99.99 € and 219.99 €. Flow comes with those plans, adding 200, 1,000, 10,000 and 25,000 credits a month on top of the 50 daily credits. What a film really costs depends on the model: from 0.7 credits per second in Omni 360p to 12.5 in Veo 3.1 Quality.

### What's the longest shot you can make in Google Flow?

Eight seconds with Veo 3.1 (Lite, Fast and Quality offer 4, 6 or 8 seconds) and ten seconds with Gemini Omni Flash 1.1. You can extend an 8 second Veo shot using Veo 3.1 Lite, but extending Omni shots is only announced so far. For editing, Flow accepts uploads of up to 60 seconds, which you trim to 30, then you edit 10 second segments. A short film gets built shot by shot, then assembled in Scenebuilder or in a real editing app.

### Can I use Google Flow videos commercially?

Flow's help center gives neither a yes nor a no. It points to Google's terms of service, to be read in full, and says Google won't claim ownership of original content you generate. No difference between free access and paid plans is shown on this point. Every output from Veo, Omni and Nano Banana carries an invisible SynthID watermark, and a visible watermark can be turned on or off in your profile settings. For work delivered to a client, have someone whose job it is check the terms.

### Does Google Flow work in Europe?

Yes. Flow is available in France and every EU country for people over 18 whose age is verified. Two features are off: editing uploaded videos, meaning using Gemini Omni to fix footage you shot yourself, and avatars. Those restrictions cover the whole European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom. Google also recommends English prompts for the best results. Generation, the agent and Scenebuilder are not on the restriction list, so you can still make and assemble shots from Europe.

### Google Flow or Runway for making a film?

Flow is cheaper to start and gives you Veo 3.1 and Gemini Omni even on free access. Runway offers more models and longer standing editing tools, with a paid plan needed to really work. Neither starts from a screenplay. My pick: Flow to learn and test an idea, Runway if you fix a lot of existing footage, especially from Europe where Flow blocks editing of uploaded videos. My Runway prices from October 25 are in the [Higgsfield vs Runway](/en/blog/higgsfield-vs-runway) comparison.

### Veo 3.1 Quality or Omni for a short film?

For shots with a recurring character, Gemini Omni Flash: it's the only Flow model that accepts references at every length, up to 10 seconds, and it costs 15 credits for a 10 second shot in 720p. Veo 3.1 Quality costs 100 credits for 8 seconds and takes no references, so keep it for shots with no character to hold, like an establishing shot or an insert. Veo 3.1 Fast sits in between at 20 credits. Choose shot by shot, after you've written your shot list, not generation by generation.
