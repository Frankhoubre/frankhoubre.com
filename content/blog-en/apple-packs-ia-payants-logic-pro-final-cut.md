Apple has just done something it had never done: **charge for AI use beyond a quota**. Since November 9, 2026, **Apple Creator Studio** subscribers can buy an "intelligence usage pack" for **$49.99** in Logic Pro once they've used up the AI included in their month. France is one of the eight countries covered.

If you edit your films, music videos or music on a Mac, this concerns you. Maybe not today, but that's the direction: the AI in your creative software is becoming a meter, like the generation credits you already know elsewhere.

What follows comes from [Apple's support page](https://support.apple.com/en-us/127888) read on November 10, and from [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/09/apple-launches-50-intelligence-usage-packs-for-select-ai-features/) (Ryan Christoffel) and [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2311500/apple-starts-offering-50-dollar-extended-usage-packs-for-ai-features-in-creator-studio/), both published November 9. I haven't bought a pack myself.

## What Apple launched

![Apple support page: customers with active Apple Creator Studio subscriptions in Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Mexico, United Kingdom and the United States can purchase intelligence usage packs to keep using intelligence features after reaching the monthly usage limit](/images/blog/apple-packs-ia-payants-logic-pro-final-cut/apple-assistance.webp)

*Source: Apple, support page on Apple Creator Studio intelligence features, captured on November 10, 2026.*

Apple Creator Studio is the subscription that bundles Final Cut Pro, Logic Pro, Pixelmator Pro and the AI features of Keynote, Pages, Numbers and Freeform. It includes a monthly AI allowance. Apple gives minimums if you only use one feature: **50 generated images**, 250 shapes, 50 presentations of about 8 to 10 slides, or presenter notes for 700 slides.

For Logic Pro's assistant, Apple gives no number: the limit depends on how complex your conversations are. Apple also says Logic Pro's assistant and image generation run on **Google Gemini**, and presentation generation on **OpenAI**.

The pack works like this, according to Apple's page:

- **One-time purchase**, not a subscription. $49.99 in the US, the only amount 9to5Mac spotted. I found no euro price.
- It's used **only once your monthly allowance is gone**.
- What's left **carries over** month to month and expires **12 months after purchase**.
- No Family Sharing, and **no refunds** unless the law requires it.
- Available in **Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Mexico, the UK and the US**.

## What about Final Cut Pro?

For now, **only Logic Pro** lets you buy a pack. In the Logic Pro menu, under Intelligence Features, then Show Usage, a Buy More Usage button appears.

## What it changes for you

The real problem isn't the price. It's **when** you find out. Running out of allowance on a render night, the day before delivery, is the worst way to pay.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** I'd rather have a pack that rolls over for 12 months than one more subscription. It's fair to people who create in bursts, like most indies. But a meter you never check is a meter that surprises you. And Apple doesn't say how much work a pack covers: $49.99 for an unknown amount, I wouldn't buy blind.

One honest caveat: this doesn't replace the video generation tools you use to make your shots. Apple's allowance covers its own features in its own apps. ## What I would do this week

1. **Open your usage tracker** in Logic Pro, or in Pixelmator Pro and Keynote if you subscribe: app menu, Intelligence Features, Show Usage. Check where you stand before you need it.
2. **Spot your peaks.** If you burn your allowance on one project a month, a pack that rolls over for 12 months can make sense. If you exceed it every month, it's a fixed cost in disguise.
3. **Keep a margin before delivery.** Don't start the heaviest AI tasks on the last day.
4. **Write down the real price** when the pack shows up in euros in your App Store, and compare it with what you already pay in credits elsewhere.

## FAQ

### What is an Apple intelligence usage pack?

It's a one-time purchase, $49.99 in the US, that lets Apple Creator Studio subscribers keep using their apps' AI features after using up their monthly allowance. Apple launched it in Logic Pro, according to 9to5Mac and Engadget on November 9, 2026.

### Is the pack available in France?

Yes. According to Apple's support page read on November 10, 2026, packs are offered to subscribers in Brazil, Canada, France, Germany, India, Mexico, the UK and the US. The euro price doesn't appear in the sources I read.

### Does it apply to Final Cut Pro?

Not yet. Only Logic Pro lets you buy a pack today. 9to5Mac expects Final Cut Pro, Pixelmator Pro and iWork to follow with the November Creator Studio updates, but Apple hasn't announced it.

### What happens to the pack if I don't use it?

It's only used once the monthly allowance runs out. What's left carries over month to month and expires 12 months after purchase. The pack can't be shared with family and isn't refundable unless the law requires it.

### What AI allowance does Apple Creator Studio include?

Apple gives monthly minimums if you use a single feature: 50 images, 250 shapes, 50 presentations of 8 to 10 slides, or presenter notes for 700 slides. For Logic Pro's assistant, the limit depends on conversation complexity. The allowance resets every month.

### Which models power Apple Creator Studio's AI?

According to Apple's support page, Logic Pro's assistant on Mac and the generation of images, shapes and presenter notes run on Google Gemini. Presentation generation in Keynote, and the slide images, run on OpenAI.
