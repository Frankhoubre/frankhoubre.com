Tired of Kling? First figure out whether your problem is the model or the checkout. If you like how Kling looks but its billing scares you, keep the model and pay someone else: Runway carries Kling 4.0, Higgsfield carries Kling 3.0. If you want a different engine, pick by shot. Seedance 2.5 for dialogue and faces that have to stay the same, MiniMax H3 (inside Hailuo) for acting on a tight budget, Vidu for 2D anime, Google Flow to try things for free. And if what's slipping away is the whole film, you need a tool that starts from the script.

I'm a co-founder of ScreenWeaver, which is on this list. Every price below was checked on November 1, 2026 on the official pages, from France, and the screenshots were taken that day. I didn't run any generations for this article: I'm comparing what the companies publish and what users say in public.

## Why people look for a Kling alternative

Kling has real strengths for fiction, and I went through them in [my Kling AI review](/en/blog/kling-ai-avis-fiction): very believable physical motion, action shots that hold up, and since October 15, Kling 4.0, which goes up to 30 seconds per generation with up to 10 keyframes. People who leave rarely complain about the picture. Their complaints are about the bill, the credits and the support.

Money comes first. On November 1, [Kling's Trustpilot page](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/klingai.com) showed a score of 1.2 out of 5 from 431 reviews, 91% of them one star, with reviews posted up to October 30. The same complaints keep coming back: credits spent on videos that ignored the prompt, fixes that cost more credits, subscriptions that are hard to cancel, support that doesn't answer. These are public testimonies with no statistical weight, but there are a lot of them and they sound alike.

Then there's the big number on the pricing grid, which only lasts a month. The monthly grid shows $6.99 for Standard in large type. That's the first month, at 30% off. After that you pay $8.80 a month, printed in small type right underneath. Same story for Pro ($25.99, then $32.56) and Premier ($144.99, then $181.99).

The big price is for the first month only; the real rate sits on the Then line." source="https://kling.ai/app/membership" capturedAt="2026-11-01" />

A more technical limit: Kling only does Kling. No Seedance to hold a face with lots of references, no Veo, no MiniMax. A fiction scene often needs two engines, one for the action shot and another for the shot-reverse-shot where two characters talk. With a Kling subscription alone, you can't do that.

And then there's anime. Kling can produce a drawn look, but it's tuned for realism first. If you're making a 2D series, there are tools built for that look, and we'll get to them.

## Keep Kling, pay somewhere else

Most lists skip this option. If you like Kling's output and only the checkout bothers you, two platforms give it to you inside their own subscription, under their own billing terms.

Runway added Kling 4.0 to its paid plans. On November 1, [its pricing page](https://runwayml.com/pricing) listed 118 credits for 5 seconds of Kling 4.0 at 1080p, down to 89 credits with a 25% discount running until November 30. Standard costs $15 a month, or $12 billed yearly, for 625 credits: about seven 5-second Kling 4.0 shots during the promo, five at the regular rate. Pro goes to $35 ($28 yearly) for 2,250 credits, and Max to $95 ($76 yearly) for 9,500 credits, with unused credits rolling over for one month.

Higgsfield, for its part, offers Kling 3.0. On November 1 its plan calculator showed "~50 Kling 3.0 videos" for 700 credits, roughly 14 credits per video. The Starter tier, at €19 a month billed yearly, gives 270 credits but only a selection of models. Plus, at €47 a month billed yearly (€59 crossed out), gives 1,200 credits and opens up the rest of the catalog.

## Seedance 2.5 in Dreamina: for faces that have to hold

This is the alternative I know best, because it's the model we use to make Lost Garden, our animated series. Seedance takes a lot of references as input, which helps enormously when a character comes back across thirty shots. For a shot-reverse-shot where two characters talk, that's where I prefer it to Kling. I put the two head to head, criterion by criterion, in [Kling vs Seedance](/en/blog/kling-vs-seedance), and the model gets a full breakdown in [my Seedance 2.5 review](/en/blog/seedance-2-5-avis).

The most direct way to use it on its own is Dreamina, CapCut's tool. On November 1 its pricing page opened on the yearly tab. Basic: €86.99 for the first year, then €144.99 a year, for 1,361 credits a month. Standard: €212.99 for the first year, then €354.99, for 3,336 credits a month, with Seedance 2.5 advertised from €0.14 per second at 720p. Advanced goes down to €0.08 per second. Paid plans remove the Dreamina watermark from downloads.

Two caveats. Dreamina's pricing page is covered by a login window when you open it without an account, and I couldn't get a readable screenshot: the numbers above come from the page text, read the same day. And the promo display is confusing: on November 1 the page still advertised an offer that "Ends October 30!". Check the final price before you pay.

Seedance is available elsewhere too: at Higgsfield, in Runway (Seedance 2.0 Pro), and in ScreenWeaver, where Seedance 2.5 is the default model. If you want Seedance plus other engines in one subscription, those are better entry points than Dreamina.

## Hailuo and MiniMax H3: acting at the lowest price

MiniMax, the Chinese company listed in Hong Kong, released its H3 model on July 31, 2026. In its Hailuo app, the model page advertises 5 to 15 second clips at 24 frames per second, up to 1440p (2K), with native stereo sound and up to 12 reference files for character, motion, camera and voice.

What catches my eye for fiction is that the page leads with performance: "Expressive Performance" and "Realistic Body Motion & Emotion" are the first two demos. It also cites the Artificial Analysis Video Arena, built from blind votes, where H3 ranked first in video editing with audio and third in image-to-video with audio. MiniMax is the one putting that ranking forward, so keep that in mind.

On price, the app doesn't show its subscriptions without a login: the subscription page shows only "From /mo" with no number. The model page does give a per-second estimate: about $0.073 to $0.120 per second at 2K depending on the plan, so $0.37 to $0.60 for a 5-second clip with no video input.

## Vidu Q4: the pick for 2D anime

If your project is a Japanese-style series or a cartoon, Vidu is the tool on this list that leans hardest into that. Its pricing page highlights five points, including "Multi-Reference Consistency" and "Smooth 2D Animation". The current model, Vidu Q4, generates 3 to 16 seconds per video on paid plans.

Prices sit close to Kling's. The free tier gives you Q4 at 540p for 4 credits per second, with 10 references a month. Standard costs $8.25 a month billed at $99 a year ($10 crossed out), for 800 credits, and a second of Q4 at 540p drops to 2 credits. Premium, at $35.42 a month billed yearly, adds 20 off-peak videos a day. I couldn't find the credit cost of 720p and 1080p on the page.

Vidu also has a Video to Anime tool: you give it a live-action clip, a reference image and a prompt, and it returns a stylized version. Handy if you want to shoot a motion reference on your phone and turn it into an animated shot.

## Google Flow: Veo 3.1 for trying things free

On Reddit, in the "Alternative to klingai?" thread on [r/KLING](https://www.reddit.com/r/KLING/), the first suggestion is Veo 3.1 Fast in Google Flow. Makes sense: no alternative gives you as much without a credit card. According to Flow's official help page, everyone gets 50 credits a day, subscriber or not. A Veo 3.1 Lite clip costs 10 credits, Fast costs 20, Quality costs 100.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-credits-2026-11-01.webp" alt="Google Flow help page in French: 50 Google Flow credits a day with or without a subscription, 200 extra credits a month with Google AI Plus, 1000 with Pro, 10000 and 25000 with the two Ultra plans" caption="Flow's credit rules on November 1 (French help page): 50 credits a day for everyone, then a monthly top-up depending on the plan." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-11-01" />

So your free day is worth five Lite shots or two Fast shots. Enough to test an idea, not to shoot an episode. Flow has its own flaws, which I listed in [my Google Flow review](/en/blog/google-flow-avis): Google models only, safety refusals on ordinary scenes, characters that drift from one clip to the next. If you leave Kling for Flow and Flow lets you down too, [the Google Flow alternatives](/en/blog/alternatives-google-flow) pick up from there.

## ScreenWeaver: when the problem is the film, not the shot

My conflict of interest is total here, so I'll stick to published facts. People often leave Kling blaming the engine, when the real blocker is elsewhere: forty shots generated one by one, with no script and no shot list, that tell nothing once you cut them together. Switching engines won't fix that.

ScreenWeaver starts from a screenplay editor in standard format, or from an import of Final Draft, Fountain, PDF or WriterDuet files, free since October 31. It builds the shot list and storyboard from that, then produces the video in the same project. The video models include Seedance 2.5 (the default), Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, MiniMax H3, Grok Imagine Video 1.5 and Wan 3.0. Every Lost Garden episode was made in it; the first one, 17 minutes long, took me 63 hours.

## When to stay on Kling

Stay on Kling if you mostly do action, stunts and complex body movement, and one engine is enough for you. Standard at $8.80 a month is still one of the cheapest subscriptions around, and the pricing page says generated content is for commercial use from that plan up. Kling 4.0 also brings things few competitors do, like 30 seconds in one generation and 10 keyframes to steer a long shot.

To limit bad surprises: pay monthly rather than yearly until you're sure, note your renewal date, and test a prompt at 720p before you launch the 1080p version. Credits lost on failed generations are the top public complaint. You won't dodge that completely, but you can make it cost less.

## FAQ

### What is the best Kling AI alternative?

Start from what bothers you. If it's Kling's billing, keep the model and go through Runway, which offers Kling 4.0 on plans from $15 a month. If you want another engine for dialogue and recurring faces, take Seedance 2.5. For 2D anime, Vidu. For acting at the lowest price, MiniMax H3. To try without paying, Google Flow and its 50 daily credits. And for a whole film, a tool that starts from the script, like ScreenWeaver, which I co-founded.

### Is there a free alternative to Kling AI?

Yes, with limits. Google Flow gives everyone 50 credits a day, which is five Veo 3.1 Lite shots or two Fast shots. Vidu has a permanent free tier on its Q4 model, but at 540p and 4 credits per second. Runway gives 125 credits once, enough to see the interface. None of these lets you produce an episode; they're for testing a style or an idea before you decide where to pay. ScreenWeaver is free for writing the script, not for video.

### Can you use Kling without a Kling subscription?

Yes. Runway includes Kling 4.0: on November 1, 2026, its pricing page listed 118 credits for 5 seconds at 1080p, or 89 credits with the 25% discount running until November 30. Higgsfield offers Kling 3.0, at about 14 credits per video according to its calculator. You then pay under those platforms' terms, not Kling's. Credit for credit, though, it costs more than a direct Kling subscription: you're paying for the convenience and the other models.

### Which Kling alternative is best for anime?

Vidu leans hardest into animation: its pricing page highlights smooth 2D animation and multi-reference consistency, and its Video to Anime tool turns a live-action clip into a stylized shot. Seedance 2.5 also works very well in anime style; it's the model we use to make Lost Garden. The choice depends on your look: Vidu for a clean, cheap 2D style, Seedance when characters have to stay identical across a long series. Test both on the same shot before committing.

### Why does Kling have such a bad Trustpilot score?

On November 1, 2026, Kling showed 1.2 out of 5 on Trustpilot from 431 reviews, 91% of them one star. The complaints cover five things: videos that ignore the prompt, credits lost on those failed generations, unexpected charges and price increases, a cancellation option that's hard to find, and support that rarely answers. These are public testimonies with no measurement behind them, and they target billing far more than image quality. That's why going through another platform makes sense if you like the model.

### Is Hailuo cheaper than Kling?

Per second, yes. The MiniMax API charges $0.08 per second at 768P and $0.13 at 2K for H3, and the Lite version drops to $0.04 at 768P. Hailuo estimates its own costs at $0.073 to $0.120 per second at 2K depending on the plan. The Hailuo app doesn't show its subscriptions without a login, so I can't compare monthly plans. Kling Standard is still very low at $8.80 a month for 660 credits, but the real cost depends on how many takes each shot needs.

### Kling or Seedance for a fiction scene?

Kling for physical motion and action, Seedance for dialogue and faces that have to stay the same. Seedance takes far more references as input, which matters when a character comes back across dozens of shots. Kling 4.0 has 30-second shots and 10 keyframes on its side. On a single scene, switching between the two depending on the shot often gives the best result, and multi-model platforms make that possible. I covered the head-to-head in my Kling vs Seedance comparison.
