If you want a Higgsfield alternative for making a film, pick by project first. For a series or a written short, you need a tool that starts from the script: ScreenWeaver or Saga. For an ad or a teaser cut by a small team, LTX Studio. For a music video or footage you want to rework, Runway. To test without paying, Google Flow and its 50 free credits a day. And if you mostly make one-off shots across lots of models, Higgsfield is often still the right tool.

I'm a co-founder of ScreenWeaver, which is on this list. I'm saying it once up here and I'll say it again when I get to it. Every price below was checked on October 18, 2026 on the official pages, and every screenshot was taken that day. I didn't run a single generation for this piece: I'm comparing what the companies publish and what users report.

## Why filmmakers look for a Higgsfield alternative

Higgsfield does one thing very well: a single subscription gets you Seedance, Kling, Veo, Nano Banana and about twenty other models, plus Cinema Studio for camera control. I covered it in [my full Higgsfield review](/en/blog/higgsfield-avis-realisateur). People who leave give four reasons, and you can check every one of them.

Money comes first. Starter costs €19 a month before VAT, and the page states plainly that there's "No difference compared to monthly". You get 270 credits and access to Seedance 2.0 Fast and 2.0 Mini, not the other Seedance models. Plus, at €59 a month or €47 on annual billing, opens every model with 1,200 credits.

<Screenshot src="higgsfield-tarifs-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Higgsfield individual plans in euros: Starter at 19 euros with 270 credits, Plus at 47 euros billed annually with 1200 credits, Ultra at 99 euros with 3000 credits, and a teams column from 65 euros per seat" caption="Higgsfield's individual plans as shown in France, annual billing. Starter doesn't get cheaper; Plus and Ultra do." source="https://higgsfield.ai/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

Then there's "Unlimited". The Plus and Ultra cards promise unlimited generations on Nano Banana 2 and Kling 3.0, but the exact wording is "7-day unlimited". Seven days. And a line at the bottom of the page warns that unlimited usage can be slowed down when traffic is high.

Refunds are the third sore spot. The official help page sums it up in one paragraph: 7 days after your first purchase, only if you haven't used a single credit, a service fee of up to 6%, and renewals are never refunded.

<Screenshot src="higgsfield-remboursement-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Higgsfield help page titled How do I request a refund, with a table of three conditions: 7 days from the initial purchase, zero credits used, initial purchase only" caption="The three conditions for a Higgsfield refund. One paid generation and you lose the right." source="https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/refunds/how-do-i-request-a-refund" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

On [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/higgsfield.ai), Higgsfield holds a 4 out of 5 (5,064 reviews on October 18), yet 18% of reviews give it one star. The newest one, posted that same day, complains that the credit cost per generation went up after the reviewer subscribed, so the same video now costs about twice as much. That's the reviewer's claim. I haven't verified it.

The last reason hits filmmakers directly. Popcorn, Higgsfield's storyboard tool, makes sequences of up to 8 consistent frames. Past eight, you chain sequences by hand.

<Screenshot src="higgsfield-popcorn-huit-images-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Official Popcorn help page describing it as Higgsfield's storyboard generator, with consistent sequences of up to 8 frames" caption="Popcorn keeps things consistent across 8 frames. A dialogue scene with shot reverse shot often needs more." source="https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/ai-models/how-do-i-use-popcorn" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

And nothing in Higgsfield starts from a written script. You type a prompt, you get a shot. For a film, someone still has to hold the text, the shot list and the continuity together, and that's the criterion I used to sort the tools below.

## ScreenWeaver: for a series or a written short

My conflict of interest peaks here, so I'll stick to facts. ScreenWeaver is a pipeline. You write the script in a standard-format editor (or import it from Final Draft, Fountain or WriterDuet), the tool generates the shot breakdown and the storyboard from the text, and then you generate the footage inside the same project. Every shot stays tied to its scene.

The video models are Seedance 2.5 by default, Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling v3.0, MiniMax H3 and Grok Imagine Video 1.5. That's far fewer than Higgsfield. Where it differs is continuity: you handle characters, sets and references shot by shot in the storyboard, before you hit generate. Every episode of Lost Garden, our animated series, was made in it, from script to production.

<Screenshot src="screenweaver-tarifs-2026-10-18.webp" alt="ScreenWeaver pricing section on the French site with three plans: Screenwriter free, Auteur at 39.99 dollars a month with 6000 credits, Studio at 149.99 dollars a month with 25000 credits" caption="ScreenWeaver's three plans. Generated breakdowns and storyboards start with Auteur." source="https://www.screenweaver.ai/fr#pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

The paid entry point is $39.99 a month before tax, roughly twice Higgsfield's Starter. There's no trial of the paid plans: signing up gives you 20 credits, and the free Screenwriter plan covers writing only (editor, sequencer, moodboards, import and export). The site doesn't publish what each generation costs in credits. Real-time collaboration is Studio only, at $149.99. And the product is young: video generation opened to all subscribers on October 8, 2026.

I'd point someone to it if they write before they shoot and they're prepping an episode or a short. If you never write a script, you'll pay for a part of the tool you won't use.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** before you compare tools on image quality, write your hardest scene, the one with two characters and an eyeline match. Then ask which tool will let you find it again in three weeks, after you've rewritten the sequence. That's where the gaps show.

## LTX Studio: for an ad or a team teaser

LTX Studio is ScreenWeaver's closest competitor, I went through it in detail in [my LTX Studio review](/en/blog/ltx-studio-avis), then head to head in [ScreenWeaver vs LTX Studio](/en/blog/screenweaver-vs-ltx-studio). It starts from an uploaded script, a concept, an image or a video, and puts the storyboard, generation, a timeline and sound in one workspace.

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-tarifs-2026-10-18.webp" alt="LTX Studio Pick your plan page in euros, annual billing: Free 0 euros with 800 one-time credits, Lite 12 euros, Standard 28 euros, Pro 100 euros a month" caption="LTX Studio's plans on annual billing. AI storyboards and the commercial license arrive with Standard." source="https://ltx.io/studio/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

In the plan comparison, AI Storyboards, Elements (characters, places and props you call by name) and the commercial use license are all ticked from Standard up, at €35 a month or €28 on annual billing, with 28,000 credits. Lite, at €15, mostly gets you generation and upscaling. Veo 3.1 only comes with Pro, at €125 or €100 annual.

Its refund window is longer than Higgsfield's: 14 days, as long as you haven't used more than 1,200 credits. On Trustpilot, though, LTX sat at 1.6 out of 5 when I checked on October 17, mostly over refused refunds.

It suits an agency or a commercial director mocking up a spot from a script someone else already wrote, who wants to share the project. For writing the film itself, LTX doesn't document a screenplay editor. If LTX is the tool you want to leave, I wrote [a separate list of LTX Studio alternatives](/en/blog/alternatives-ltx-studio).

## Saga: for a screenwriter on a tight budget

Saga (CyberFilm AI, formerly writeonsaga.com) promises the same chain as ScreenWeaver: plot, characters, script, storyboard, video, edit. Its strength sits upstream. The free plan gives you plot development, character archetypes and act structure, and the homepage puts structure templates for features and TV front and center.

<Screenshot src="saga-promesse-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Saga homepage with the headline The Solution to Explode your Creative Energy and the promise of going from a logline to visuals in a single platform" caption="Saga's pitch: start from a logline, write the scenes and generate visuals in one place." source="https://www.createsagas.com" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

It has the lowest entry price on this list for a tool that starts from the script: Premium at $19.99 a month with 40 Saga credits. The FAQ translates those credits into "up to 40 images or 4 videos per month". Four videos a month is enough to preview a scene, not to produce a short.

<Screenshot src="saga-tarifs-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Saga pricing: Free at 0 dollars, Premium at 19.99 dollars a month with 40 credits, Ultra-Premium at 149.99 dollars marked coming soon, and extra credits at 9.99 dollars for 25" caption="Saga's Premium is still $19.99 on October 18. Ultra-Premium is still marked coming soon." source="https://www.createsagas.com/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

Three caveats, all from the [official FAQ](https://www.createsagas.com/faq). No refunds, period. The Premium trial lasts 3 days with 8 images max. And the same FAQ announces Premium going to $29.99 "in Spring 2026" in one place and "Summer 2026" in another, while the pricing page still shows $19.99. I found almost no independent user feedback on the tool.

Saga fits a screenwriter who wants to structure a story and see a few frames of their scenes for $20. For producing a whole episode, the video quota won't cut it.

## Google Flow: for starting without paying

Flow, Google's video studio, has something no other tool here offers: 50 credits a day, with or without a subscription. The official help page lists what each model costs: 10 credits for a Veo 3.1 Lite clip, 20 for Veo 3.1 Fast, 100 for Veo 3.1 Quality (8 seconds). With the daily allowance, that's five Lite clips, or two Fast clips, and zero Quality clips.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-credits-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Google Flow help page in French: 50 credits a day with or without a subscription, 200 extra credits a month with Google AI Plus, 1000 with Pro, 10000 and 25000 with the two Ultra plans" caption="Flow credits: 50 a day for everyone, then a monthly top-up depending on your Google AI plan." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

To assemble clips, Flow has Scenebuilder: you add clips to a scene, reorder them, trim the start and end, preview the whole sequence and download it. That's plenty for a first edit test.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-scenebuilder-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Google Flow help on Scenebuilder: organize several clips into a sequence, reorder them, trim the start and end, preview and download a scene" caption="Scenebuilder assembles clips you've already generated. It doesn't break a script into shots." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16935718?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

Only Google models are on offer (Veo 3.1 and Gemini Omni Flash), so no Kling and no Seedance. I found no screenplay editor and no shot breakdown anywhere in the help center. And in Europe, the help center says editing uploaded videos and avatars aren't available. If you want to get the most out of Veo, I wrote a method for [briefing Veo like a cinematographer](/en/blog/google-veo-3-brief-realisateur-plan-sequence).

Flow works well if you're starting out, if you want to see a shot before you pull out a card, or if you make short shots with no recurring character.

## Runway: for a music video or footage to rework

Runway plays a different game from Higgsfield. It generates too (Gen-4.5, plus third-party models like Kling 3.0 and Nano Banana Pro listed on its pricing page), but its real specialty is editing existing footage: Aleph 2.0 changes a video you already have, at 140 credits per 5 seconds.

<Screenshot src="runway-tarifs-2026-10-18.webp" alt="Runway pricing page on annual billing: Free 0 dollars with 125 credits, Standard 12 dollars with 625 credits, Pro 28 dollars with 2250 credits, Max 76 dollars with 9500 credits" caption="Runway's individual plans on annual billing. Only Max rolls unused credits over, for one month." source="https://runway.com/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-18" />

Run the numbers before you subscribe. Standard, at $15 a month ($12 annual), gives you 625 credits. Runway does the math itself: 52 seconds of Gen-4.5. For a three-minute music video, you'll move up to Pro fast ($35 or $28, 2,250 credits) or to Max ($95 or $76, 9,500 credits, one month of credit rollover).

It's the right pick for a music video mixing shot footage and generated shots, or a scene where you want to change the light, the set or an object without regenerating everything. For writing a film, Runway gives you nothing Higgsfield doesn't. I put the two head to head, including the cost of a 5-second shot, in [my Higgsfield vs Runway comparison](/en/blog/higgsfield-vs-runway).

## When to keep Higgsfield

Keep it if you mostly make one-off shots (social ads, effects, look tests) and you want to compare Seedance, Kling and Veo without juggling accounts. No alternative on this list gives you that many models under one subscription. Keep it too if Cinema Studio works for you on camera, lens and grading.

Look elsewhere if your credits go into shots you throw away because a character's face changed. Fix the method first: what's missing is almost always a shot breakdown. I explained [why characters change from one scene to the next](/en/blog/pourquoi-personnages-changent-chaque-scene-ia), and how to [prepare a shot breakdown before you generate](/en/blog/preparer-decoupage-technique-avant-generation-video-ia).

## Which tool for which project

| Your project | What I'd pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| An animated series or a short with dialogue | ScreenWeaver | Script, breakdown and shots stay linked |
| A first screenplay on a small budget | Saga | Structured writing even on the free plan, Premium at $19.99 |
| An ad or teaser from a script you were handed | LTX Studio (Standard) | AI storyboard, Elements, commercial license |
| A music video with real footage | Runway | Editing with Aleph 2.0 |
| A test with no credit card | Google Flow | 50 credits a day |
| One-off shots across lots of models | Higgsfield | The widest catalog |

Nothing stops you from combining two tools: write and break down in one, shoot certain shots in the other. Just don't pay for two subscriptions that do the same job.

## FAQ

### What's the best Higgsfield alternative?

Pick by what you're making. For a film with a written story, a tool that starts from the script will do more for you than another catalog of models: ScreenWeaver if you want to produce the shots inside the same project, Saga if money is tight and you mostly write. For an ad built from a script you were handed, LTX Studio on the Standard plan. For a music video mixing real footage and generated shots, Runway and its Aleph editing tool. For one-off shots across lots of models, Higgsfield is hard to beat, and leaving it doesn't make much sense.

### Is there a free alternative to Higgsfield?

Google Flow is the most generous day to day: 50 credits a day, with or without a subscription, which buys five Veo 3.1 Lite clips or two Veo 3.1 Fast clips. Runway gives you 125 credits once when you sign up, LTX Studio 800 credits once. ScreenWeaver and Saga have free plans built around writing: you can write your screenplay without paying, but video generation needs a subscription. None of these free plans is enough to produce a full short film. They're for testing before you choose.

### Which Higgsfield alternative is the cheapest?

Looking at the first paid tier on October 18, 2026, Runway Standard is the cheapest on annual billing ($12 a month, $15 monthly), followed by LTX Studio Lite (€12 annual, €15 monthly), then Saga Premium at $19.99. Do the real math, though: Runway's 625 Standard credits come to 52 seconds of Gen-4.5 by Runway's own count, and Saga's 40 credits to about 4 videos a month according to its FAQ. Compare what one usable minute of your project costs you instead.

### Does Higgsfield refund a subscription?

Only in one specific case, according to its official help page as read on October 18, 2026: a first purchase, within 7 days, without using a single credit. A service fee of up to 6% applies and the subscription is canceled immediately. Renewals, automatic or manual, are never refunded. The window can be longer where the law in your country requires it. Compare that with LTX Studio, which generally accepts requests within 14 days up to 1,200 credits used, and Saga, which offers no refunds at all.

### What's the Higgsfield equivalent for a whole film?

There's no exact equivalent, because Higgsfield is a model aggregator built for making shots. For a whole film you need what it doesn't do: a script, a shot breakdown, continuity held from one shot to the next. Three tools on this list start from a text. ScreenWeaver and Saga let you write it inside the tool; LTX Studio imports it. All three advertise a storyboard built from the script. Going by their public pages, ScreenWeaver is the only one that keeps each shot tied to its scene all the way to production, and it's also the most expensive entry point of the three.

### Can I use Seedance or Kling without Higgsfield?

Yes. Seedance 2.5 is available in ScreenWeaver, among others, where it's the default model, and Runway lists Seedance in its credit cost table. Kling 3.0 shows up in Runway, in LTX Studio from Standard up, and in ScreenWeaver. Kling also has its own platform, kling.ai, which was announcing Kling 4.0 on October 18, 2026. If you only use one of these models, compare a direct subscription with an aggregator, and look at the credit cost of that specific model, not just the plan price.
