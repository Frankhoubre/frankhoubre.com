For a director, Runway earns its price in 2026 on one specific job: fixing shots you already have. Aleph 2.0 swaps a jacket, a set or a season on a 30-second clip and leaves everything else alone, and the Premiere Pro, After Effects and DaVinci Resolve plugins put it right on your timeline. Generating a film from scratch is a harder sell. Gen-4.5 costs 12 credits per second, a minute runs $7 to $17 depending on the plan, and the whole platform is leaning toward advertising (Agent, Runway Ads, App Studio). My take: an excellent retouching and finishing tool, a decent but pricey generator, and customer service rated 1.1 out of 5 on Trustpilot.

I co-founded ScreenWeaver, a tool that goes from screenplay to shots. Runway isn't built into it, so it's an indirect competitor. I didn't run a single generation in Runway for this piece. Everything comes from official pages checked on October 29, 2026 without an account (pricing, changelog, the Gen-4.5, Aleph 2.0, Workflows and Agent pages, the app in guest mode), from Trustpilot, and from math on the published rate card. When it's my opinion, I say so.

## Gen-4.5: how good is the in-house video model

Gen-4.5 is the one generation model you can't get anywhere else. At launch, Runway said it topped the Artificial Analysis text-to-video leaderboard with 1,247 Elo points.

<Screenshot src="runway-gen-4-5-classement-2026-10-29.webp" alt="Runway Gen-4.5 research page with the launch text and a chart of the Artificial Analysis Text to Video leaderboard as of November 30, 2025: Gen-4.5 at 1247, Veo 3 at 1226, Kling 2.5 at 1225, Veo 3.1 at 1223, Sora 2 Pro at 1206" caption="The chart Runway published, frozen at November 30, 2025. The rivals listed are Veo 3, Kling 2.5, Veo 3.1 and Sora 2 Pro. Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5 and Gemini Omni didn't exist yet." source="https://runway.com/research/introducing-runway-gen-4.5" capturedAt="2026-10-29" />

Check the date: November 30, 2025. Since then Kling 4.0 shipped on October 15, 2026 with 30-second shots, and Seedance 2.5 takes more references and longer outputs. Runway's "number one" is an eleven-month-old snapshot. Its Google ad still leads with it today.

The same page promised to bring every existing control mode to Gen-4.5: image to video, keyframes, video to video. Starting from a still is still the most reliable way to hold a frame, and I walk through the method in my [Runway tutorial on animating a still image](/en/blog/tutoriel-runway-gen-3-animer-image-fixe-plan-film).

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** never generate your final shot straight in Gen-4.5. Check the motion in Gen-4 Turbo first: for 625 credits, the pricing page lists 104 seconds of Turbo against 52 of Gen-4.5, so half the price. If the face already melts in Turbo, your source image is the problem, and you found out for half the cost.

What I don't know: how stable a face stays across several Gen-4.5 shots. Runway publishes no numbers on it, and recent Trustpilot reviews complain about exactly that, characters and outfits shifting between generations.

## Aleph 2.0: fix a shot without reshooting it

Aleph 2.0 is an editing model, and it's the main reason to pay for Runway if you make films: you hand it an existing video, describe what should change, and it touches only that.

<Screenshot src="runway-aleph-2-fonctions-2026-10-29.webp" alt="A new way to modify video section of the Aleph 2.0 page with four features: edit one frame to update the whole video, change a specific element and keep the rest, apply an edit across multiple shots, work with up to 30 seconds of video at 1080p" caption="Aleph 2.0's four promises: one edited frame carried through the whole video, one element changed without touching the rest, edits across several shots in the same clip, and up to 30 s at 1080p." source="https://runway.com/product/aleph-2" capturedAt="2026-10-29" />

Runway's examples are all advertising: sneakers turned red, graffiti added to a wall, an ad redone as a winter version. Move them onto a film set. Your actor wore a green jacket and the art director wanted red. An anachronistic object sits on a shelf. The scene was shot in summer and the edit puts it in November. Each of those used to mean a trip back to post or a reshoot.

In Edit Studio, you see your edit on a still frame before you launch the video generation.

<Screenshot src="runway-aleph-2-edit-studio-2026-10-29.webp" alt="Runway Edit Studio interface shown on the Aleph 2.0 page: a frame of a small white truck in a parking lot, an instruction to dig out the ground around the truck, a column of prompt versions on the right and the Generate video button" caption="Edit Studio as shown on the official Aleph 2.0 page: you edit a frame, compare versions in the right column, then generate the video. The credit counter belongs to Runway's demo account." source="https://runway.com/product/aleph-2" capturedAt="2026-10-29" />

My read: that preview is Runway's best idea this year, because it goes after the real cost of AI video, failed takes. On the official screenshot, the preview selector reads Nano Banana Pro, billed at 20 credits per 2K image on the rate card. An Aleph video costs 140 credits for 5 seconds. Seven bad previews cost about the same as one bad video.

On the limits side, thirty seconds at 1080p covers a short scene, not a full sequence, and nothing points to 4K output. I also found no public data on how Aleph handles faces in close-up, where any flaw shows.

## Workflows, plugins and App Studio: the workshop around the models

Workflows is Runway's node editor, launched October 24, 2025. You wire inputs (text, image, video), models and LLMs that rewrite your prompts, then run the whole chain or one node at a time.

<Screenshot src="runway-workflows-nodes-2026-10-29.webp" alt="Build your own Workflow section of the Runway Workflows page with three node types: Input Nodes for image, video and text, Media Model Nodes with Gen-4, Gen-4 Turbo, Flash 2.5 and Veo 3.1, and LLM Nodes to rewrite prompts" caption="The three node families in Workflows. The page still lists Gen-4, Gen-4 Turbo, Flash 2.5 and Veo 3.1, lagging behind the editor, which also offers newer models. Every model or LLM node uses credits." source="https://runway.com/workflows" capturedAt="2026-10-29" />

The sample workflows include a "Storyboard Creator" and an "Animatic Generator." For an ad cut into ten formats, that saves real time. For a film, a workflow is still a chain of operations: it doesn't know scene 12 follows scene 11, or that the character changed clothes in between. It runs what you wired.

Since September 8, Runway runs in a panel inside Premiere Pro and After Effects, and since September 23 inside DaVinci Resolve. In Premiere and After Effects, per the September 8 changelog entry, you restyle a timeline shot with Aleph 2.0 and the result drops back into place in the sequence. For an editor, that's where AI belongs: shot by shot, no export and reimport.

App Studio, released October 26, builds small creative apps from a description. I noted it without judging it: nothing on the page talks about fiction.

My [Higgsfield vs Runway comparison](/en/blog/higgsfield-vs-runway) runs the same math for a 5-second shot, credit for credit. These numbers assume every take is a keeper, which never happens. For a 3-minute short in Gen-4.5, I count four generations per kept shot (my working assumption, not a Runway figure). That's 8,640 credits. Pro gives you 2,250, so you'd need almost four months. Sound, editing and grading come on top: my piece on [what an AI short film costs in 2026](/en/blog/combien-coute-court-metrage-ia-2026) covers those lines.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** if you're taking Runway on a project longer than a month, pick Max over Pro for the rollover. One stalled week of production on Pro, and the credits you didn't get to use vanish on your billing date.

## What Runway users say

In the recent reviews on [the Trustpilot page](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/runwayml.com), I mostly see credits burned on failed videos, black screens included, outputs that ignore the prompt (characters and outfits that change), and support that answers with automated messages. On Google, the results for the French query also surface a year-old r/runwayml Reddit thread where the top answer just says to go with Kling.

My read: the rating mixes two problems. One is billing, and the pricing page gives you every rule, non-rolling credits included. The other is failed generations, and Runway only refunds credits when a generation ends in an error, not when you dislike the result. For an Aleph edit, the image preview limits the damage. For a Gen-4.5 generation from scratch, you pay for every attempt.

## Who Runway is for, and when to pick something else

Runway fits if you already cut in Premiere, After Effects or Resolve and want to fix shots without going back to set. It also fits ads and branded content, since the Agent, Brand Kits and Runway Ads are built for that. And it fits if you want Gen-4.5 plus third-party models without stacking subscriptions.

Look elsewhere if you mainly want to generate a film from scratch on a small budget: 625 credits on Standard is 52 seconds of Gen-4.5 a month, before your first bad take. If you're making a series with recurring characters, continuity gets prepared upstream with sheets and references, and my guide to [keeping the same character from shot to shot](/en/blog/garder-meme-personnage-video-ia) applies to Runway like the rest. And if you just want to try it for free, the 125 one-time Free credits are gone in about ten seconds of Gen-4.5.

## FAQ

### Is Runway free?

Partly. The Free plan costs $0 and gives you 125 credits once, which don't expire, on a selection of models, with 5 GB of storage. At the Gen-4.5 rate of 12 credits per second, that's about 10 seconds of video. Enough to see the interface and run a test, not a project. The pricing page only shows "No watermarks" from the Standard plan up. To actually work, you need a paid plan, starting at $15 a month on monthly billing or $12 a month on annual billing.

### How much does Runway cost?

On October 29, 2026, the pricing page lists Standard at $15 a month monthly ($12 annual) with 625 credits, Pro at $35 ($28) with 2,250 credits, and Max at $95 ($76) with 9,500 credits that roll over for a month. There's also a Team plan for 2 to 9 people and an Enterprise plan on quote. Prices are in dollars. Extra credits come in batches of 1,000 minimum and never expire, but the per-credit price isn't shown on the public page.

### How many credits does a Runway video cost?

Each model has its own rate. Gen-4.5 costs 60 credits for 5 seconds, so 12 per second. Aleph 2.0, the editing model, costs 140 credits for 5 seconds. Kling 4.0 at 1080p is 118 credits for 5 seconds, 89 with the discount running until November 30, 2026. Seedance 2.0 Pro at 1080p climbs to 160 credits for 4 seconds. So a minute of Gen-4.5 costs 720 credits, more than the Standard plan's 625, and that's before any failed takes.

### How do I cancel Runway?

I couldn't check the steps without an account: the pricing page doesn't describe them, and its FAQ only mentions the Plans & Billing page in the dashboard, for buying credits. If you downgrade, your current plan stays active until the end of the period and the unused balance goes toward your next bill, per the pricing FAQ. On refunds, the official help center says they're at Runway's discretion, within 30 days of a purchase or renewal, and only if the subscription has barely been used. Write down your renewal date when you sign up.

### Runway or Kling for a short film?

Kling for generating shots from scratch, especially with motion: Kling 4.0 outputs shots of 3 to 30 seconds and costs less per credit. Runway for fixing and finishing: Aleph 2.0 changes one element of an existing shot without touching the rest, and the plugins bring it into Premiere, After Effects or Resolve. You can combine them, since Kling 4.0 is available inside Runway with 25% off until November 30. My take: generate in whichever engine suits the shot, fix it in Runway.

### Can I use Runway videos commercially?

Yes. The official help article on usage rights, updated September 2, 2026 and rechecked on October 25, says generated content belongs to the user with no non-commercial restriction from Runway: monetized YouTube, social media, festivals, advertising. What you put into your generations is still on you: real faces, brands, protected works. For client delivery, Standard at minimum sidesteps the watermark question, since it's the first plan to show "No watermarks."

### Is Runway Gen-4.5 still the best video model?

Runway says so based on the Artificial Analysis leaderboard as of November 30, 2025, where Gen-4.5 beat Veo 3, Kling 2.5, Veo 3.1 and Sora 2 Pro. That ranking is almost a year old. Kling 4.0, released October 15, 2026, and Seedance 2.5 weren't on it. Gen-4.5 is still a very good model, and the only one exclusive to Runway. For a director, the useful question is shot by shot: which engine holds this motion, this face, this length, at the best price.
