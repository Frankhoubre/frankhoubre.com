If your project starts from an idea, a client brief or an image, and you need a finished ad or music video this week, go with LTX Studio on the Standard plan. If your project is a written film, with dialogue and rewrites, and you want every shot to stay tied to the scene it came from, go with ScreenWeaver. On entry price the two sit a few euros apart. The real gap is where your screenplay lives, how a team works together, and what happens when you ask for your money back.

I co-founded ScreenWeaver. So you're reading a competitor's take, and I'd rather you know that before the first table. To keep the bias in check, I stick to what both companies write on their own pages, checked on October 19, 2026, and I flag every time something is my opinion. I didn't run a single generation in LTX Studio for this piece.

## Screenplay first or concept first

LTX Studio offers four ways in on its homepage: a script, a concept in a few lines, an image or a video.

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-points-de-depart-2026-10-19.webp" alt="Choose your starting point section of the LTX Studio homepage with four entries: From Script, With a Concept, From an Image and From a Video" caption="LTX Studio's four starting points on October 19. The script is one door among four, and LTX has you upload it." source="https://ltx.io/studio" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

Look at the verb on the first line: "Upload your script". LTX imports text written somewhere else and turns it into scenes and a storyboard. Neither the homepage, the pricing page nor the help center describes a screenplay editor in industry format, with scene headings, dialogue and version history. For an ad or a music video, nobody will miss it: the brief fits on a page and rarely gets rewritten.

ScreenWeaver goes the other way. It opens on a screenplay editor, with a beat board and story elements (characters, locations, props), and writing stays free with no time limit. You can also import a Final Draft, Fountain or WriterDuet file. The shot breakdown and the storyboard come next, built from your scenes.

<Screenshot src="screenweaver-pipeline-2026-10-19.webp" alt="Write, Storyboard, Produce section of the ScreenWeaver homepage showing the Storyboard view of a demo project: character sheets, shot S3 in the rain and the screenplay on the right" caption="The Storyboard view on our homepage: characters on the left, the shot in the middle, the screenplay scene on the right. All three live in the same project." source="https://www.screenweaver.ai/fr" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

My take, and it's a competitor's take: the deciding question is how many times your text will change. Lost Garden, our series, was made entirely in ScreenWeaver, from script to production, and the 17-minute first episode took me 63 hours. On a project that size, a storyboard kept in a separate tool goes stale the moment the text moves, and nobody tells you.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** before you compare tools, count your drafts. If your last project went through more than three rewrites, look for a tool where the screenplay and the shots live in the same file. If it went through one brief and two rounds of client notes, a concept-first tool will get you there faster.

## Storyboards and character consistency

Both tools generate a storyboard from your text, and both put it behind a paid plan: Standard at LTX, Auteur at ScreenWeaver.

What matters is what happens to that storyboard when the text changes. Our FAQ puts it this way: images and panels are tied to scene headings, scenes and beats, and when the script moves, the visual layer stays aligned with the structure you wrote. On the LTX side, I couldn't find a help article explaining what happens to shots you already generated when you re-upload an edited script. LTX may handle it perfectly well, but nothing public describes it, so test it first during your 14 days.

For consistency from shot to shot, LTX has a really good idea called Elements: you create a character, prop, location, style or color once, then call it by name in any prompt. I covered it in [my full LTX Studio review](/en/blog/ltx-studio-avis). ScreenWeaver carries characters, sets and references from shot to shot inside the project. Either way, the principle is the one I lay out in [why characters change from one scene to the next](/en/blog/pourquoi-personnages-changent-chaque-scene-ia): consistency comes from references, never from luck.

## Models: who generates what

LTX has something we'll never have: its own model. LTX-2.5, released on August 11, 2026, runs on every plan, free included, and Lightricks publishes LTX-2 as open source with its weights. Partner models cost more: Kling 3.0 and Seedance 2.0 come with Standard, Veo 3.1 only with Pro, at €125 a month. For images, LTX offers Nano Banana 2 and Pro, FLUX.2 Pro, ChatGPT Images 2.0 and Z-Image.

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-modeles-par-offre-2026-10-19.webp" alt="Models by plan table on the LTX Studio pricing page, columns Free, Lite, Standard, Pro and Enterprise, Veo 3.1 checked only for Pro and Enterprise" caption="LTX's model comparison on October 19. The crosses in the first two columns show the price of partner models: Standard at minimum, Pro for Veo 3.1." source="https://ltx.io/studio/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

ScreenWeaver has no in-house model. For video it runs Seedance 2.5 by default, Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0 (image to video, text to video and Motion Control), MiniMax H3, Grok Imagine Video 1.5 and Wan 3.0, added on October 18. They're available from the Auteur plan, with no extra tier for Veo. For images there are six models, including GPT Image 2.5, Nano Banana 2.1, Seedream 5.0 Pro and FLUX 3.

For a director, the concrete difference is Seedance. LTX has 2.0, ScreenWeaver has 2.5, which is the default model in our pipeline. If you'd rather compare engines before picking a platform, my [roundup of AI video tools](/en/blog/meilleurs-outils-ia-video) is the place to start.

## Pricing, credits and usage rights

On paper, LTX Standard at €35 and ScreenWeaver Auteur at $39.99 before tax land a few euros apart. LTX even drops to €28 a month billed yearly, which we don't offer.

Standard is tagged Popular, and it really is the plan that unlocks everything a film needs." source="https://ltx.io/studio/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

<Screenshot src="screenweaver-tarifs-2026-10-19.webp" alt="ScreenWeaver plans on October 19, 2026: Screenwriter free forever, Auteur 39.99 dollars a month with 6000 credits, Studio 149.99 dollars a month with 25000 credits" caption="Our three plans the same day. No yearly option and no paid trial: the free way in is the screenplay editor." source="https://www.screenweaver.ai/fr" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

Don't compare 28,000 credits with 6,000. The two units have nothing in common, and neither LTX nor we publish the cost of a generation on our sites. Our terms say the cost shows up in the app before you launch each generation. At LTX, your balance shows at the top of the app. Without generating the same shot in both tools, nobody can tell you which one costs less per minute, and I won't make that number up.

The license matters far more. At LTX, Free and Lite are for personal use only: no clients, no sponsored content, no video you get paid for. The commercial license starts at Standard. Our terms don't split rights by plan: Outerframe Studio, the company behind ScreenWeaver, claims no ownership of what you generate and assigns you any rights it might hold, subject to third-party rights and the terms of the models used. In practice, LTX's Lite plan at €15 is useless if you work for clients.

One last credit detail: in both tools, unused plan credits don't roll over to the next month. At ScreenWeaver, credits from packs you buy on top don't expire with the month.

## Refunds: where LTX is more flexible

Most negative reviews of LTX are about money. On [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ltx.studio), LTX.Studio had a 1.6 rating from 79 reviews on October 19. Among the latest you'll find a cancellation that didn't go through properly, a refund turned down after a few hours of use, and a promised music video that the tool caps at 20 seconds per shot. Those are reviews, not established facts. Here's the written rule.

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-remboursement-2026-10-19.webp" alt="FAQ on the LTX Studio pricing page opened on What is your refund policy: refund within 14 days if usage does not exceed 1200 credits" caption="LTX's rule: 14 days after payment, and no more than 1,200 credits used. The help center adds that renewals aren't refunded." source="https://ltx.io/studio/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

1,200 credits out of Standard's 28,000 is about 4% of the month. The [refund policy help article](https://help.ltx.io/hc/en-us/articles/37289542565650-Refund-Policy) says the rule only covers a first purchase, and that renewals and upgrades are never refunded. That's not much room to judge a production tool, and it explains some of the reviews.

Our own terms are stricter on one point, and I'm not going to hide it. At ScreenWeaver, the 14-day withdrawal right only covers consumers. If you haven't used a single credit, you get a full refund. If you have, the refund shrinks accordingly, down to zero if you used everything. A professional, and our terms count anyone producing or selling audiovisual work commercially as one, has no withdrawal right.

<Screenshot src="screenweaver-retractation-cgv-2026-10-19.webp" alt="Section 7 of the ScreenWeaver terms, French version, withdrawal right for consumers only, full refund with no credit used and reduced according to credits consumed" caption="Our terms dated October 8, 2026, section 7, French version. Read the last line of the section: professionals have no withdrawal right." source="https://www.screenweaver.ai/fr/terms" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

A freelancer who pays for Auteur on a client project and gets cold feet after three days will be treated better by LTX's 1,200-credit rule than by our terms. Our terms also say a generation that didn't turn out the way you wanted doesn't get its credits back. So start with the writing, which is free, and only subscribe once your screenplay is ready to become shots.

On reputation, ScreenWeaver had a 4.0 from 4 reviews on Trustpilot the same day. That's far too few to mean anything, and Trustpilot notes that we haven't invited customers to review us recently.

## Working on the same film as a team

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-collaborateurs-aide-2026-10-19.webp" alt="LTX help article Adding collaborators to your project: Pro plans support up to 3 collaborators, Enterprise unlimited, each collaborator works from their own plan's credits" caption="LTX's help center: three collaborators on Pro, unlimited on Enterprise, and each collaborator works from their own plan's credits and features." source="https://help.ltx.io/hc/en-us/articles/33650434265362-Adding-collaborators-to-your-project" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

At LTX, you need the €125 Pro plan to add collaborators with edit rights, three per project. Each one has their own account and spends their own credits. Beyond that, it's Enterprise, on a quote.

At ScreenWeaver, real-time collaboration is on Studio, at $149.99 a month before tax: unlimited seats, shared workspaces, Owner, Editor and Viewer roles. For a writer and director duo, LTX Pro is cheaper. For a crew of five on a series, LTX sends you to Enterprise and a sales call, while Studio has a listed price.

## Editing, finishing and what happens after the render

After generation, LTX goes further than we do, and for ads that counts. The studio has a timeline, sound design, dubbing, captions, a Brand Kit, SDR to HDR conversion and upscaling. You can finish a video without leaving the tool.

ScreenWeaver stops at the footage: you generate shots tied to the storyboard, with Topaz video upscaling, then cut in your usual editor. If you already have an editor, or editing software you know well, you lose nothing. If you want everything in one window, LTX wins this one outright.

## Running your project from Claude or ChatGPT

ScreenWeaver has an MCP server: you connect your account to Claude, ChatGPT, Claude Code, Cursor or VS Code, and your assistant can read and search your screenplay, rewrite a scene while keeping the old version in history, import a file or create shots in the storyboard. The page lists 22 tools, available on the Auteur and Studio plans.

<Screenshot src="screenweaver-mcp-2026-10-19.webp" alt="ScreenWeaver MCP page in French: your screenplay and storyboard in Claude and ChatGPT, 22 tools, sign in with your account without an API key" caption="Our MCP page on October 19 (French version). The assistant prepares shots and prompts, but image and video generation always starts in the app." source="https://www.screenweaver.ai/fr/mcp" capturedAt="2026-10-19" />

Two limits to know: image and video generation can't be launched from the MCP, and OCR on scanned pages and automatic panel detection on comic pages cost credits. On the LTX side, Lightricks offers an API for its models, but I couldn't find any documented way to run an LTX Studio project from an assistant.

## LTX Studio or ScreenWeaver: which one for your project

Pick LTX Studio for an ad, a teaser, a music video or brand content, especially if you start from a concept and want to cut, score and caption in one place. Go straight to Standard: Lite gives you no Elements, no generated storyboard and no right to deliver to a client. And if you care about its in-house model or local generation with LTX Desktop, ScreenWeaver won't give you either.

Pick ScreenWeaver for a short film, a series or a pilot where the text matters and is going to change. Write for free first, move to Auteur once your screenplay is ready to become shots, and keep your editing software at hand. If more than three people work on the same project, look at Studio before LTX Enterprise.

One case where I'd skip both: you just want standalone shots from lots of different models, with no project behind them. An aggregator will do that job better, and I compared the options in [my piece on Higgsfield alternatives](/en/blog/alternatives-higgsfield-film). To see the steps any pipeline has to cover, whatever the tool, read [the script, storyboard, production workflow from A to Z](/en/blog/pipeline-ia-script-storyboard-production-de-a-z).

## FAQ

### Which one is free, ScreenWeaver or LTX Studio?

Both have a free plan, but they do different jobs. ScreenWeaver's Screenwriter plan gives you the full screenplay editor, the beat board, moodboards, Final Draft and PDF import and export, with no time limit, plus 20 credits when you sign up. Generated storyboards and video production need the Auteur plan. LTX Studio's Free plan gives you 800 credits once, access to the LTX-2.5 and LTX-2.3 models and blank storyboards, strictly for personal use. For writing, ScreenWeaver is free. For trying video generation without paying, LTX gives you more.

### Which one is cheaper for a short film?

Nobody can answer that with real numbers today. LTX Standard costs €35 a month, or €28 billed yearly, for 28,000 credits. ScreenWeaver Auteur costs $39.99 a month before tax for 6,000 credits. But the two credit units don't compare, and neither company publishes the cost of a shot on its website. The only real number I can give comes from our own production: episode 2 of Lost Garden, 21 minutes long, cost about $1,850 in generation. To compare, generate the same shot in both tools and watch how much each balance drops.

### Can I use the videos for a client?

At LTX Studio, yes, from the Standard plan. Free and Lite are limited to personal use, which rules out clients, sponsored content and any video you get paid for. At ScreenWeaver, the terms don't reserve commercial use to a plan: Outerframe Studio claims no ownership of what you generate and assigns you any rights it might hold. In both cases you stay responsible for the rights to whatever you feed the tool, and for the terms of the third-party models used. A face or a brand you aren't allowed to use doesn't become free because an AI generated it.

### Can I import an existing screenplay?

Yes on both sides, with a difference. LTX Studio lets you upload your script to generate scenes and a storyboard automatically, but it doesn't document a screenplay editor to keep working on it inside the tool. ScreenWeaver imports Final Draft, Fountain and WriterDuet files, and the built-in assistant can read a PDF, a scan or a photo of a page. The text lands in an industry-format editor where you keep writing, and the shot breakdown is generated from those scenes. Export goes to PDF and Final Draft. There's no Fountain export.

### Which one has the best video models?

Each wins on different models. LTX is the only one with its own model, LTX-2.5, available even for free, and Lightricks publishes LTX-2 as open source. For Veo 3.1, LTX asks for the Pro plan at €125 a month, while ScreenWeaver offers it from Auteur. For Seedance, LTX has version 2.0 and ScreenWeaver has 2.5, its default model. ScreenWeaver also has MiniMax H3, Grok Imagine Video 1.5 and Wan 3.0, which LTX doesn't list. For images, LTX gives you more choice. Both Lightricks and we keep the right to change the list.

### Which one should a team pick?

For two or three people, LTX Pro at €125 a month allows three collaborators per project, you included, but each person needs their own account and spends their own plan's credits. For a bigger team, LTX points you to Enterprise, on a quote. ScreenWeaver Studio, at $149.99 a month before tax, gives you real-time collaboration, unlimited seats and Owner, Editor and Viewer roles, with 25,000 credits a month. The right pick mostly depends on team size and on who generates the most.

### Does LTX Studio refund more easily than ScreenWeaver?

For a professional, yes. LTX usually refunds a first purchase requested within 14 days, as long as you've used fewer than 1,200 credits, whoever you are. ScreenWeaver applies the 14-day withdrawal right to consumers only: full refund with no credit used, reduced according to the credits consumed otherwise. A professional has no withdrawal right with us. In both tools, renewals aren't refunded and a disappointing generation doesn't give its credits back. Use the free plans before you pay.
