Leaving LTX Studio, or thinking twice before signing up? Pick the alternative by what you write. For a short film or a series with dialogue, use a tool where the script lives inside the software: ScreenWeaver or Saga. For an ad brief you want an agent to break down and cut for you, look at Morphic. To block your cameras in 3D before you generate anything, Autodesk Flow Studio. For consistent storyboard frames without paying for a full pipeline, Popcorn on Higgsfield. To try things for free, Google Flow. And if you mostly make ads cut by a small team, LTX is often still the best tool.

I'm a co-founder of ScreenWeaver, which is on this list and competes head to head with LTX. Every price below was checked on October 24, 2026 on the official pages, from France, and every screenshot was taken that day. I didn't run a single generation for this piece: I'm comparing what the companies publish and what users say in public.

## Why people look for an LTX Studio alternative

LTX Studio gets a lot right, and I said so in [my full LTX Studio review](/en/blog/ltx-studio-avis): one workspace for storyboard, generation, timeline and sound, an in-house model (LTX-2.5) on every plan, and Elements that let you call a character by name. The people who leave, or never sign up, keep raising the same points, and you can check each one on the pricing page.

Most of it comes down to the Standard plan. A storyboard filled in from your script ("AI Storyboards") only exists from Standard up, at €35 a month or €28 billed yearly, with 28,000 credits. The free plan gives you 800 credits once and "Blank Storyboards", meaning empty boards. Lite, at €15, adds upscaling, editing tools and no watermark. The commercial license, Elements, Kling and Seedance also start at Standard. And Veo 3.1 only shows up on Pro, at €125 a month.

The free plan only gives you empty boards: storyboards generated from a script start at Standard." source="https://ltx.io/studio/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

On refunds, the pricing FAQ says requests are "typically granted" within 14 days of payment, as long as you haven't used more than 1,200 credits. On a 28,000-credit Standard plan, that's a little over 4% of the month.

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-remboursement-2026-10-24.webp" alt="What is your refund policy question expanded in the LTX Studio FAQ: refunds typically granted within 14 days of payment if usage does not exceed 1200 credits" caption="LTX's refund rule: 14 days and 1,200 credits at most. It's the rule that comes up most in negative reviews." source="https://ltx.io/studio/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

On [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/ltx.studio), LTX.Studio showed a 1.6 rating from 79 reviews on October 24, with a crushing one-star bar. Trustpilot adds two things worth knowing when you read that number: the company hasn't invited customers to review recently, which usually skews toward unhappy ones, and it replied to 92% of negative reviews. The newest review, posted six days earlier, describes cancelling before the end of the first month and then getting charged again. That's one customer's account, and I haven't verified it.

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-trustpilot-2026-10-24.webp" alt="LTX.Studio Trustpilot page with a 1.6 rating from 79 reviews, a mostly one-star distribution, a note that the company has not asked for reviews recently and a 92% reply rate to negative reviews" caption="79 reviews is a small sample for a tool this size. Then there's the script itself. LTX imports a script written somewhere else. No public page describes a screenplay editor in standard format, or what happens to your shots when you rewrite a sequence. For a director who writes, that's often the real reason to look elsewhere.

## ScreenWeaver: when the script has to stay the source

My conflict of interest is at its peak here, so I'll stick to published facts. ScreenWeaver starts from a screenplay editor in standard format (or a Final Draft, Fountain or WriterDuet import), generates the shot list and storyboard from that text, then produces the video in the same project. Every shot stays tied to its scene, which LTX doesn't document anywhere.

<Screenshot src="screenweaver-tarifs-2026-10-24.webp" alt="ScreenWeaver pricing section in French: Screenwriter free forever with screenplay editor and beat board, Auteur at 39.99 dollars a month with 6000 credits and generated shot list, Studio at 149.99 dollars with real-time collaboration" caption="ScreenWeaver's three plans on October 24 (French version of the site). Writing stays free; shot list, storyboard and video start with Auteur." source="https://www.screenweaver.ai/fr" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

The video models are Seedance 2.5 by default, Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, MiniMax H3, Grok Imagine Video 1.5 and Wan 3.0. Veo 3.1 comes with Auteur, where LTX keeps it for Pro. Every episode of Lost Garden, our animated series, was made in it, from script to production; the first one, 17 minutes long, took me 63 hours.

The entry paid plan costs $39.99 a month before tax, more than LTX Lite and about the price of its Standard. There's no paid-plan trial: signing up gives you 20 credits. The credit cost of each generation isn't published. Real-time collaboration is Studio only, at $149.99. And LTX has things we don't: an open model, local generation, a proper Enterprise offer.

I'd point someone to it if they write before they shoot and know they'll rewrite. I put the two tools side by side, criterion by criterion, in [ScreenWeaver vs LTX Studio](/en/blog/screenweaver-vs-ltx-studio).

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** before you leave any tool, export everything that's yours: your characters' reference images, approved shots, and above all the latest version of the script. When you switch software, those references save you a week. The prompts won't.

## Saga: write before you pay

Saga (CyberFilm AI, formerly writeonsaga.com) chases the same chain as LTX but starts much earlier: plot, characters, structure, then script, storyboard, video and editing. The free plan covers plot development, character archetypes, three-act structure, limited AI help and a video editor.

<Screenshot src="saga-tarifs-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Saga pricing grid: Free at 0 dollars for plot and characters, Premium at 19.99 dollars a month with full scriptwriting, AI storyboarding and 40 credits, Ultra-Premium at 149.99 dollars marked coming soon" caption="Saga Premium is still $19.99 on October 24, and Ultra-Premium is still coming soon." source="https://www.createsagas.com/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

Premium, at $19.99 a month, opens full scriptwriting, the chatbot, AI storyboarding and video, with 40 Saga credits. The FAQ spells out what that buys: "up to 40 images or 4 videos per month". With four videos a month, you get to watch one scene move, then wait for next month to see the rest.

<Screenshot src="saga-faq-remboursement-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Saga FAQ answer: refunds are not offered, a 3-day free Premium trial with up to 8 images, Premium at 19.99 dollars, and up to 40 images or 4 videos per month" caption="Saga's FAQ on price and refunds: there aren't any, and the trial lasts 3 days." source="https://www.createsagas.com/faq" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

The FAQ is blunt: "Refunds are not offered". The Premium trial lasts 3 days, with 8 images max. If you're leaving LTX over a refused refund, know that before you enter a card number. I found almost no independent user feedback on Saga.

Saga suits a solo screenwriter who structures the story for free and buys a few shots of key scenes for $20. To produce an episode, the quota runs dry in the first week.

## Morphic: an agent that breaks down the script and cuts

Morphic is the closest alternative to LTX in spirit, and the only one here that runs the LTX model itself, among others. Its comparison page (written by Morphic, so read it as a pitch) describes an agent, Copilot, that turns an attached script into storyboard images, launches the generations and cuts the multitrack timeline in Compose. Characters are saved as "References" so they stay the same from shot to shot.

<Screenshot src="morphic-vs-ltx-studio-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Morphic vs LTX Studio comparison table published by Morphic, French version: Copilot editing, Compose multitrack timeline, plain-language workflows, 3D Scene framing, live collaboration from Pro, AI storyboards from a script" caption="Morphic's own comparison (French version). It gives LTX the edge on audio-to-video and pitch decks, and claims 3D framing and agent editing for itself." source="https://morphic.com/fr/resources/compare/morphic-vs-ltx-studio" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

As a director, I'd look at 3D Scene first, on paid plans only: you place mannequins and cameras, the tool warns you if you cross the 180-degree line, then you export the frame or the shot as a reference. That's closer to blocking on set than to a camera-move preset.

<Screenshot src="morphic-tarifs-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Morphic Simple pricing page, yearly billing: Basic 19 dollars a month with 2400 credits, Standard 24 dollars with 3625 credits, Pro 45 dollars with 6350 shared credits, Max 170 dollars with 24650 credits" caption="Morphic's plans billed yearly. The free tier caps at 20 credits with limited models." source="https://morphic.com/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

Billed yearly, Basic is $19 a month for 2,400 credits, Standard $24 for 3,625, Pro $45 for 6,350 shared credits, Max $170. I didn't check monthly prices. The free tier gives you 20 credits at most, enough to look around. An Education plan costs $4 a month with a school email. Morphic says credit use varies by operation, with no rate card on the pricing page. Like LTX, there's no screenplay editor: you bring your script.

Morphic fits an agency or a creator who gets a brief, wants it broken down and cut fast, and likes steering by chat.

## Autodesk Flow Studio: block your cameras before you generate

Autodesk Flow Studio (formerly Wonder Studio) comes from the visual effects world. Since August 2026, every plan, free included, has a 3D editor (characters, cameras, sets) and a node Canvas that generates images, video and audio with third-party models. You block the scene in 3D, choose the axis and the lens, then generate from that setup.

<Screenshot src="autodesk-flow-studio-tarifs-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Official Autodesk Flow Studio pricing guide: Education and Free at no cost, Lite at 10 dollars a month or 7 yearly with 2100 credits, Standard at 45 or 30 dollars with 6000 credits, Pro at 95 or 64 dollars with 12000 credits" caption="Flow Studio's plans from the guide Autodesk published on September 28, 2026, still live on October 24." source="https://blogs.autodesk.com/media-and-entertainment/2026/09/28/autodesk-flow-studio-pricing-and-subscription-guide/" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

The official guide lists Free at $0 with 300 credits a month, but 720p watermarked exports. Lite costs $10 a month or $7 billed yearly (2,100 credits, 1080p without a watermark), Standard $45 or $30 (6,000 credits), Pro $95 or $64 (12,000 credits). Eligible students over 18 and educators get free access. The same guide mentions a 15-day return policy on monthly plans and 30 days on yearly ones, with no credit threshold. Scenes export to Maya, Blender, Unreal or USD depending on the plan.

Flow Studio doesn't write or break down a script. It's for staging, and you need to be comfortable in 3D. If you came to LTX for its camera controls and want to go further, it's the right tool. If you want your script broken into shots for you, look elsewhere.

## Popcorn on Higgsfield: consistent frames, one at a time

If what you really used in LTX was the storyboard, Popcorn is worth a look. Higgsfield's help center calls it its storyboard generator: it makes sequences of up to 8 frames where the face, lighting and mood hold from one frame to the next. It takes up to 4 reference images (a portrait, a prop, a location), which you call by number in the prompt.

<Screenshot src="higgsfield-popcorn-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Higgsfield How do I use Popcorn help page dated August 2, 2026: Popcorn creates coherent sequences of up to 8 frames, in Manual, Auto or Instadump mode" caption="Popcorn holds consistency across 8 frames. A dialogue scene cut shot reverse shot often needs more." source="https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/ai-models/how-do-i-use-popcorn" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

Each frame then works as a start frame for Kling or Veo, and the help page suggests chaining the last frame of one clip into the next. That works for a short sequence. Past 8 frames you chain sequences by hand, and nothing starts from a written script: you write one prompt per frame. I covered the tool's strengths and limits in [my Higgsfield review](/en/blog/higgsfield-avis-realisateur), and what to use instead for a whole film in [Higgsfield alternatives](/en/blog/alternatives-higgsfield-film).

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** whatever the tool, build your character sheet before the first frame: three views of the face, the outfit, a palette. Eight consistent frames are useless if frame nine, generated a week later, starts from a different reference. I walk through the method in [keeping the same character from shot to shot](/en/blog/garder-meme-personnage-video-ia).

## Google Flow: test without a credit card

Google gives everyone 50 Flow credits a day, subscribed or not. A Veo 3.1 Lite shot costs 10, a Fast shot 20, a Quality shot 100, according to the official help page. Your free day is five Lite shots or two Fast ones. Quality stays out of reach.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-credits-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Google Flow help page in French: 50 Google Flow credits a day with or without a subscription, plus 200 a month with Google AI Plus, 1000 with Pro, 10000 and 25000 with the two Ultra plans" caption="Flow credits on October 24 (French help page): 50 a day for everyone, then a monthly top-up depending on your Google AI plan." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

Without a subscription, the help page warns that video may be unavailable at peak hours, roughly 2 to 5 pm UTC. And Flow only offers Google's models, Veo 3.1 and Gemini Omni Flash. I found no screenplay editor or shot list in the help center: Scenebuilder assembles clips you've already generated.

Flow makes a good test bench for checking that an idea holds up in images before you pay anyone.

## What about Katalist?

Katalist shows up on a lot of "script to storyboard" lists. In October 2026, its homepage talks about something else: reviving ads that performed, swapping a product into a video, rolling out variants per SKU. The script import button is still there, but the whole pitch targets marketing teams.

<Screenshot src="katalist-accueil-2026-10-24.webp" alt="Katalist homepage with the headline Give your winning creatives new life and the line Surface your best-performing ads, swap a new product in, scale across every SKU" caption="Katalist's homepage on October 24: the storyboard tool has turned toward performance advertising." source="https://www.katalist.ai" capturedAt="2026-10-24" />

If you need a storyboard for a film, I wouldn't put it at the top of the list today. If you make product ads at scale, that's become its specialty.

## When LTX Studio is still the right call

Keep LTX if you make ads, music videos or teasers, often start from a concept rather than a dialogue script, and want generation, timeline and sound in one place. No tool on this list combines an open in-house model (LTX-2), local generation with LTX Desktop, audio-to-video and pitch decks. In that case, go straight to Standard, skip Lite: without generated storyboards, Elements and a commercial license, you're paying for half the tool.

Move on if you write scenes you'll rewrite, if a 1,200-credit refund cap feels too tight to test anything, or if you need Veo 3.1 without paying €125 a month. Either way, get your shot list ready before you switch: [planning your shot list before generating](/en/blog/preparer-decoupage-technique-avant-generation-video-ia) keeps you from repeating the same mistake somewhere else.

## FAQ

### What is the best LTX Studio alternative?

Pick by what you make. For a film or a dialogue series, a tool where the script is written inside the software holds continuity better when you rewrite: ScreenWeaver or Saga. For ads broken down and cut by an agent, Morphic is the closest to LTX, and it even runs the LTX model. For 3D staging, Autodesk Flow Studio. For free testing, Google Flow and its 50 credits a day. I'm a co-founder of ScreenWeaver, so keep that in mind when you read this ranking.

### Is there a free alternative to LTX Studio?

Yes, with limits. Google Flow gives everyone 50 credits a day, which is five Veo 3.1 Lite shots. Autodesk Flow Studio offers 300 credits a month with the 3D editor, but exports in 720p with a watermark. Saga and ScreenWeaver have free plans limited to writing: plot and structure at Saga, a full screenplay editor at ScreenWeaver. None of these free tiers generates a full storyboard from a script, and neither does LTX: its free plan only gives you empty boards.

### Can you get a refund from LTX Studio?

The official FAQ says refunds are typically granted within 14 days of payment, if you haven't used more than 1,200 credits. Past that, your request may be refused, and that's the most common complaint on Trustpilot, where LTX had a 1.6 rating from 79 reviews on October 24, 2026. Before subscribing, note your payment date and watch your credit counter during the first days. Cancelling stops the renewal; your plan runs until the end of the paid period.

### Which LTX Studio alternative actually starts from a script?

Two tools on this list have a built-in screenplay editor: ScreenWeaver and Saga. ScreenWeaver then generates the shot list and storyboard from the text and keeps each shot tied to its scene, from $39.99 a month before tax. Saga puts the weight on early writing (plot, archetypes, structure) with a $19.99 Premium, but only 4 videos a month. Morphic and LTX read an imported script; Higgsfield, Flow Studio and Google Flow start from a prompt or a 3D scene.

### Is Morphic better than LTX Studio?

On paper, Morphic covers more ground: an agent that breaks down a script and cuts the timeline, 3D framing that checks the 180-degree line, and the LTX model available alongside others. But that comparison comes from Morphic, and I haven't tested it. LTX keeps audio-to-video, pitch decks and an in-house model published as open source. On price, Morphic starts at $19 a month billed yearly for 2,400 credits, LTX at €12 billed yearly for 8,000 credits, but their credits don't compare one to one.

### Can you keep the LTX model if you leave LTX Studio?

Yes, two ways. Lightricks publishes its LTX model family as open source, with LTX Desktop for generating locally on your own machine. And Morphic offers the LTX video model in its model picker, next to other engines: one shot can run on LTX and the next on another model, in the same project. The engine comes with you. The LTX Studio interface (Elements, Storyboard Builder, Flows) stays with Lightricks.
