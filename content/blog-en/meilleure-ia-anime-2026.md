If you want to animate the shots of an anime in 2026, start with Seedance 2.5. It gives you 30 seconds per generation and 50 references per request, and it's the model behind the production of my series Lost Garden. Vidu Q4 comes right after: it's the tool most focused on 2D animation, and the cheapest per second. Kling earns its place when you need to copy a specific movement. For drawing your characters before you animate them, niji is still the reference on the image side. None of these tools makes an episode on its own. You need a method around them.

## What an AI has to hold to make an anime

A good demo shot proves nothing. A series needs the two-hundredth frame to show the same knight, the same blue mushroom glow and the same pace of movement as the first one. Lost Garden's process page puts it in its own words: AI outputs get sorted, fixed or thrown out as soon as they break the bible.

So I ranked the tools on five criteria, in this order:

1. **Holding the style from shot to shot.** Line, flat colors, shadow color. An anime that slides from cel shading to smooth 3D in three shots is done for.
2. **Keeping the same character.** Face, hair, costume. This is where you lose the most hours. I cover it in depth in [how to keep the same character from shot to shot](/en/blog/garder-meme-personnage-video-ia).
3. **Readable motion.** Japanese animation lives on held poses and snappy moves. A model that makes everyone float misses the genre.
4. **Voice and sound.** Dialogue, lip sync, ambience. Some models do it, some don't.
5. **The real price per second.** Once the first-month promo is over.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** before you compare models, draw your character sheet (front, profile, back, three expressions) and keep it in one folder. Every tool in this ranking does better when you feed it the same reference, and you can switch tools without starting over.

## 1. Seedance 2.5: the model for a series

Seedance 2.5 is a ByteDance Seed model. You never buy it directly: you get it through Dreamina or CapCut, through the BytePlus API, or through a tool that runs it. The official page describes a model that generates picture and sound together, built to tell a story in 30 seconds, with a better read of reference videos.

<Screenshot src="seedance-2-5-page-officielle-2026-10-26.webp" alt="Official Seedance 2.5 page on ByteDance Seed with a dancer in a red dress and the Get API, Try now and Compare now buttons" caption="ByteDance Seed's page: 30 seconds per generation, extendable twice, with audio and video generated together." source="https://seed.bytedance.com/en/seedance2_5" capturedAt="2026-10-26" />

It ranks first because of the reference count, a criterion generic rankings almost never look at. The BytePlus docs list 50 references per request (30 images, 10 videos, 10 audio tracks), against 15 for version 2.0. For a series, that means you can hand the model the character sheet, the set, the palette and a voice sample in the same generation. Nothing helps more to keep a character's face the same. I go through the rest in my [full Seedance 2.5 review](/en/blog/seedance-2-5-avis).

Know the limits before you pay. The model refuses real faces as references, which won't bother an anime but rules out rotoscoping actors. Output tops out at 1080p on the API. And among the audio languages BytePlus lists, Japanese is there and French isn't, so a French dub needs voices generated separately.

The cheapest way in is Dreamina. On October 26, the page showed a 98% discount on the first month of the Basic plan.

<Screenshot src="dreamina-tarifs-seedance-2026-10-26.webp" alt="Dreamina plans grid with Basic at 0.27 euro for the first month then 13.99 euros, Standard, Advanced and Ultra, with Seedance 2.5 from 0.01 euro per second banners" caption="Dreamina shows €0.27 for the first month, then €13.99 a month. The second number is the one your budget needs." source="https://dreamina.capcut.com/seedance/seedance-2-5" capturedAt="2026-10-26" />

After that first month, Basic goes back to €13.99 with 1,361 credits, Standard to €31, Advanced to €138 and Ultra to €450. Dreamina estimates Basic covers 19 ten-second videos a month. Nineteen 10-second takes is a little over three minutes of raw footage, before you throw out the bad ones. For a 17-minute episode, do the math now.

## 2. Vidu Q4: the most 2D-minded of the bunch

Vidu is built by ShengShu Technology. It's the tool in this list that leans hardest into anime: its pricing page puts "Smooth 2D Animation" among its five headline features, and it has a dedicated page for turning filmed footage into anime.

<Screenshot src="vidu-q4-chiffres-2026-10-26.webp" alt="Vidu Q4 Highlights block with four figures: 4K max resolution, 16 seconds max length, 15 reference images and 3 reference audios" caption="Vidu Q4 in four numbers: 4K, 16 seconds, 15 images and 3 voice references per generation." source="https://www.vidu.com/vidu-q4" capturedAt="2026-10-26" />

Q4 takes 1 to 15 reference images (characters, sets, style) and up to 3 voice references to clone a character's voice with lip sync. The page names martial arts, running and combat as moves it holds better, with fewer distortions. For a shōnen with action scenes, that's the line that matters. Length caps at 16 seconds, about half of Seedance 2.5.

Price is its other edge. The pricing page loaded in full on October 26, which it didn't when I checked on the 16th.

## 3. Kling: when you need to copy a movement

Kling, from Kuaishou, puts "All-New Kling 4.0" at the top of its site. For anime work, I keep it mainly for Motion Control.

<Screenshot src="kling-motion-control-2026-10-26.webp" alt="Kling AI Motion Control page titled Precision Character Animation From Reference Video with an illustration of a red-haired woman in a jumpsuit walking next to a tiger" caption="Motion Control puts the movement of a reference video onto a still image. Kling's FAQ says it works on human, anime and 3D characters." source="https://kling.ai/feature/ai-motion-control" capturedAt="2026-10-26" />

You film a gesture (a sword guard, a run, a bow), give Kling the image of your character, and it reproduces the movement and expressions. The page says it plainly: "Works on humans, anime & 3D". The limits are written down too. One main subject per video, no sound or lip sync (Kling points you to Video 3.0 for that), and results that get worse when the subject in the video and the one in the image have nothing in common. My step-by-step on the previous version is in my [Kling 3 workflow for smooth animation](/en/blog/workflow-kling-3-animation-fluide-realiste).

The downside is well documented. On Trustpilot, Klingai has 1.2 out of 5 from 425 reviews, unclaimed profile.

<Screenshot src="kling-trustpilot-2026-10-26.webp" alt="Klingai Trustpilot profile with a 1.2 out of 5 score from 425 reviews, the Unclaimed profile label and a review summary focused on credits and billing" caption="Trustpilot's summary talks about prompts that get ignored, wasted credits, and billing and refund problems." source="https://www.trustpilot.com/review/klingai.com" capturedAt="2026-10-26" />

## 5. Voice: ElevenLabs, or the models' native audio

An anime without believable voices falls flat. You have two options. Keep the native audio of the video models: Seedance 2.5 speaks Japanese, Vidu Q4 clones up to three reference voices. Or record or generate the voices separately and line them up in the edit.

For a dub in a language the model doesn't speak, the second option is close to mandatory. For that, look at [ElevenLabs](https://try.elevenlabs.io/4zj8lv8ns556) (affiliate link), which generates and clones voices. A voice made outside the video model stays the same across every episode, whatever model you use that week.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** record or generate your dialogue BEFORE you animate. A shot timed to a line that already exists cuts together in one take. The other way round, you end up regenerating shots to gain two seconds of mouth.

## FAQ

### What is the best AI to make an anime in 2026?

To animate the shots of a series, Seedance 2.5 is my first pick. It accepts 50 references per generation and produces up to 30 seconds at once, which helps keep the same character from shot to shot. Vidu Q4 is the best alternative if your style is very 2D or your budget is tight, at about $0.02 a second at 540p on the Standard plan. Kling copies a filmed movement with Motion Control, and niji draws the characters before you animate them. No tool makes a full episode alone: you combine image, video, voice and editing.

### Is there a free AI to make an anime?

There are free plans, but none of them is enough for an episode. Vidu has a free plan that burns 4 credits per second at 540p with Q4, twice the paid rate, and caps references at 10 a month. Dreamina shows a button to start for free, and its Basic plan cost €0.27 for the first month on October 26, 2026, before going to €13.99. That's enough to learn and test a style. To produce several minutes, plan a budget, and always read the price after the promo.

### How do you keep the same character in an AI anime?

Build a sheet before you generate anything: front, profile, back, expressions, costume, palette. Then give those images as references for every shot. Seedance 2.5 takes up to 30 of them, Vidu Q4 up to 15. Also describe the character with the same words in every prompt. When a shot drifts, check that no reference is missing before you blame the model. Avoid face close-ups until the sheet is solid, that's where the gaps show the most.

### Can AI turn filmed footage into anime?

Yes. Vidu has a Video to Anime tool that takes a 1 to 8 second filmed clip and turns it into an anime version, guided by a reference image and a prompt. Kling Motion Control works the other way: it takes the movement from your video and applies it to your character's image, and its page says it works on anime characters. Two limits to know: one main subject on Kling, and no sound. For a dialogue scene, you'll add the voices in the edit.

### How long does it take to make an AI anime episode?

On Lost Garden, episode 1 (17 minutes) took me 63 hours and episode 2 (21 minutes) took 75 hours, with $1,852 of generation. Those are my published numbers, for all the work on an episode: writing, storyboard, generation, sorting and editing. Your time will depend mostly on your prep and on how many takes you're willing to throw away. A first short of two or three minutes is a better starting point than a full episode.

### Can I use an AI-made anime commercially?

On Vidu, yes: its pricing page says generated content can be used commercially on paid plans. For the others, read your subscription terms before you publish or sell. Watch the style too: closely imitating an existing studio or series is a rights problem whatever the tool. Seedance itself drew legal threats from US studios in 2026 over this. Build your own world, it's safer and more interesting.

### Which tool to adapt a manga or webtoon into an anime?

You need a tool that keeps the layout. Manga panels are already a storyboard: the job is turning them into shots, with the same characters, then animating them. ScreenWeaver, which I co-founded, imports comic, manga or webtoon pages, splits the panels and prepares the shots to animate with Seedance 2.5. You can also do it by hand: each panel becomes a reference image, you animate panel by panel in Vidu or Seedance, then you edit.
