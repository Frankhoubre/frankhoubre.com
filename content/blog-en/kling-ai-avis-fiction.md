For fiction, Kling is still one of the strongest engines when things move. Kling 4.0 shipped on October 15, 2026: up to 30 seconds per generation, 10 keyframes, 15 references. Video 3.0 already cuts a scene into several shots in a single generation. The downside is well known. The subscription costs 25 to 36% more from the second month, credits expire after 31 days, and Trustpilot sits at 1.2 out of 5. My take: a good engine for action shots and short dialogue scenes, a bad pick if you have no budget for failed takes.

I co-founded ScreenWeaver, which also runs Kling as one of its video models. So Kling is both a competitor and a supplier for me. I ran no generations in Kling for this review. Everything comes from the official pages I read on October 28, 2026 without an account: the subscription grid, Kling's user guides, Trustpilot, and math done from the published rate cards. When it's my opinion, I say so.

## What Kling offers in October 2026

Kling is Kuaishou's video tool, launched in 2024. Since October 15, the app opens straight on Kling 4.0, with a promo banner and a prompt box set by default to "Omni reference", 720p, 5 seconds, 16:9.

Several model generations live side by side. The official release notes lay out the timeline: Kling 3.0 rolled out on January 31, 2026, Motion Control 3.0 on March 4, native 4K output on April 23, Kling 3.0 Turbo on June 17, then Kling 4.0 on October 15. The guides still list Video 3.0, Video 3.0 Omni, O1, Avatar 2.0 and 2.6.

Around the models, Kling has built a suite: Canvas, a node workspace with an agent that drafts a storyboard (launched January 29, 2026), Motion Control to transfer motion from a video onto an image, an avatar generator, lip sync and an API. ## Kling 4.0 on a fiction scene: what the spec sheet says

Kling published the official 4.0 guide on October 26, with a capabilities table up top.

<Screenshot src="kling-4-specifications-2026-10-28.webp" alt="Core Capabilities and Specifications table from the Kling 4.0 guide: text to video, image to video, first and last frames, up to 10 keyframes, Omni Reference, 3 to 30 s duration, 720p 1080p 4K, 10-bit HDR, 21:9 16:9 1:1 9:16 aspect ratios, stereo audio" caption="Kling 4.0 on paper: 3 to 30 s per generation, 10 keyframes, 21:9 and stereo audio. Earlier in the same guide, 10-bit HDR is marked coming soon." source="https://kling.ai/quickstart/kling-4-model-user-guide" capturedAt="2026-10-28" />

Thirty seconds in one generation is a whole short film scene. The 10 keyframes let you pin the beats: she walks in, stops, turns, he arrives. And 21:9 finally gives you a cinema frame without cropping. References go up to 15 per generation, including 10 images, 5 videos totalling 30 seconds, and 7 elements. The guide warns that the two entry points, Omni Reference and Keyframes, can't be combined in one generation. You steer with references or with keyframes, pick one.

Two features are still listed as coming soon in the guide itself: 10-bit HDR and extending a video up to 2 minutes. I left them out of my verdict.

One line in the guide stopped me. Kling tells you to avoid negative instructions, except two: "no subtitles" and "no background music". The model may add subtitles and music by default. On a fiction shot, that means burned-in text you never asked for and music fighting your mix. A Trustpilot review from October 17 complains about exactly that: weird lettering all over the video and thousands of credits gone.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** end every Kling 4.0 fiction prompt with "no subtitles, no background music". Two seconds of typing, and you won't pay for an extra take because of text you never wanted.

What's missing to judge it properly: I found no public data on how stable a face stays over 30 seconds, or on the share of usable takes. The first reviews online mostly come from competing platforms that resell Kling, so I don't use them as evidence.

## Video 3.0 multi-shot: a dialogue scene in one generation

Before 4.0, Kling had already shipped something very useful for fiction: Video 3.0 multi-shot. The guide describes two modes. With "Multi-Shot", the model breaks the scene into shots on its own, based on your prompt. With "Custom Multi-Shot", you set the number of shots and the length of each, then describe every shot.

<Screenshot src="kling-video-3-multi-plans-2026-10-28.webp" alt="Kling Video 3.0 guide describing the Multi-Shot and Custom Multi-Shot modes, with two interface screenshots: a prompt split into Shot 1, Shot 2, Shot 3 and a panel where each shot has its own 7 s and 8 s duration" caption="Video 3.0 Custom Multi-Shot: one field per shot, one duration per shot. A mini shot list, inside the prompt box." source="https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-video-3-model-user-guide" capturedAt="2026-10-28" />

Kling itself names shot-reverse-shot and cross-cutting among the techniques the model understands. For a two-character scene, you write "shot 1, wide of the corridor; shot 2, close-up on her; shot 3, reverse on him", give it 15 seconds total, and get back a scene that's already cut, with audio and lip sync for each character.

My take: this is Kling's most interesting feature for a director, more than 4.0's duration. It has a built-in limit, though. The shot list lives in the prompt box, shot by shot, scene by scene. If scene 12 changes in your script, nothing updates those shots, and you rewrite the prompt by hand.

## Keeping the same character from shot to shot

For continuity, Kling has its Element Library: you build a character, prop or set from 2 to 4 images or a video, then call it back in your generations. Creating an element is free. How many you can keep depends on your plan.

<Screenshot src="kling-elements-limites-2026-10-28.webp" alt="Kling Element Library guide FAQ: 30 elements without a subscription, 50 on Standard, 150 on Pro and Premier, 500 on Ultra, free creation, multi-view completion at 5 credits after 3 free uses per day" caption="Element Library limits: from 30 without a subscription to 500 on Ultra. Above your plan's limit, you can't create or edit elements." source="https://kling.ai/quickstart/klingai-element-library-3-user-guide" capturedAt="2026-10-28" />

In Video 3.0, once you've uploaded a first frame or a first and last frame pair, you can bind up to 3 extra elements, as long as they appear in those frames. In 4.0, the cap goes up to 7 elements per generation. For a short with three characters and two sets, 50 elements is plenty. A series is another story.

Watch one quiet rule: if your plan drops or expires, your elements still work, but above your new plan's limit you can't create or edit any. Most continuity work happens before Kling anyway: character sheet, angles, lighting. I cover it in my guide on [keeping the same character across AI video shots](/en/blog/garder-meme-personnage-video-ia).

In real life you throw takes away. Take a 3-minute short at 1080p with sound, and an assumption of mine: four generations for every shot you keep. That's 8,640 credits. Pro, with 3,000 credits, won't cover it; Premier at 18,000 credits covers two shorts a month. At Premier's regular price, that short comes to about $87 of generation, not counting the daily credits subscribers get, whose amount isn't shown.

And Kling 4.0? Its credit cost in the app doesn't appear on any public page I could read. The only official number comes from the API price list, as a preview.

## What users say about Kling

The public rating is very bad. On Trustpilot, the Klingai profile shows 1.2 out of 5 from 427 reviews on October 28. The profile is unclaimed, and Trustpilot notes the company doesn't invite customers to review, so the reviews may not be representative.

<Screenshot src="kling-trustpilot-2026-10-28.webp" alt="Trustpilot page for Klingai with a 1.2 out of 5 rating from 427 reviews, the Unclaimed profile label, a dominant one-star bar and the summary of recent reviews" caption="Trustpilot on October 28: 1.2 out of 5, 427 reviews, overwhelmingly one star. People who review unprompted are often the unhappy ones." source="https://www.trustpilot.com/review/klingai.com" capturedAt="2026-10-28" />

Three complaints keep coming back in the recent reviews on [the Trustpilot page](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/klingai.com). Failed, distorted or off-topic videos that still eat credits. Hard cancellation: in a review from October 25, support sends the user from the app to the website, and after fifteen minutes the reviewer still can't find how to cancel. And support that doesn't answer, including about generations covered in stray text.

My take: output quality is up for debate, billing much less so, since Kling's own pages document it. A first-month price up front, auto-renewal, credits that expire. Every forgotten month costs you full price. Set a calendar alert three days before each renewal.

## Who Kling is for, and when to pick something else

Pick Kling if your film has action, chases, bodies in motion, cameras that follow. If you shoot short dialogue scenes and Video 3.0 multi-shot saves you editing. If you need long 20 or 30 second shots without a cut. For the prompting method that gets the most out of Kling's motion, my [Kling 3 workflow for fluid animation](/en/blog/workflow-kling-3-animation-fluide-realiste) still holds on Video 3.0.

Pick something else if you want to try without paying: Google Flow gives 50 credits a day. If your budget can't absorb failed takes, or you're on a series with a script still moving, you need a tool that keeps text and shots linked. And if commercial use is planned, forget the free plan: it doesn't allow it.

## FAQ

### Is Kling AI free?

Partly. The Basic plan is $0, and non-subscribers get free credits each month, claimed when they log in and valid for 31 days. The subscription page doesn't show how many. The free plan has real limits: one video per generation, 30 elements at most, no watermark removal and no commercial use. Enough to try a fiction prompt. To deliver a film or an ad, you need a paid plan, from $6.99 for the first month and then $8.80 a month on Standard.

### How much does Kling AI cost per month?

On October 28, 2026, the monthly grid shows Standard at $6.99 for the first month then $8.80, Pro at $25.99 then $32.56, Premier at $135 then $181.99 for 18,000 credits, and Ultra+ at $402 then $544.99 for 57,000 credits. The big numbers are first-month prices with a 30 to 35% discount. The regular price is written in smaller type. A yearly plan with 40% off and a Business plan also exist. Prices are in dollars and the grid doesn't mention tax.

### How many credits does a Kling video cost?

For Video 3.0, the official rate is per second: 12 credits at 1080p with audio, 9 at 720p with audio, 8 and 6 without audio, plus 2 credits per second if you turn on voice control. A 5-second 1080p shot with sound costs 60 credits, a minute 720 credits. For Kling 4.0, the in-app cost isn't published on any public page I read. On the API, the preview lists 1 unit per second at 720p and 1.5 at 1080p, at $0.14 per unit.

### What is the maximum length of a Kling video?

With Kling 4.0, one generation runs from 3 to 30 seconds, according to the official guide published on October 26, 2026. Video 3.0 goes from 3 to 15 seconds, with multi-shot inside the same generation. Kling also announces extending a 4.0 video up to 2 minutes, but the guide marks it as coming soon. So for a short film, you still build scene by scene and then edit in a tool like DaVinci Resolve.

### Can I use Kling videos commercially?

Yes, on a paid plan. The official FAQ lets paid subscribers use, reproduce, distribute and modify generated content for commercial purposes, on one condition: not using it to build a product or service that competes with Kling. The grid shows the free Basic plan doesn't include commercial use. Kling also leaves you responsible for image rights, copyright, trademarks and labelling AI content. For client work, read the paid services agreement.

### Is Kling AI reliable?

On the tech side, Kling is one of the most advanced video engines for motion. On the service side, Trustpilot shows 1.2 out of 5 from 427 reviews as of October 28, 2026, with recurring complaints about credits eaten by failed videos, hard cancellation and support that doesn't reply. The profile is unclaimed and Kling doesn't invite customers to review, which mostly brings in the unhappy ones. My advice: monthly plan, never yearly at first, and an alert before every renewal.

### Kling 4.0 or Kling 3.0 for a short film?

Kling 4.0 for long shots: up to 30 seconds, 10 keyframes to place the beats, 15 references, 21:9 and stereo audio. Video 3.0 for dialogue scenes split into several shots, with a published rate that lets you budget before you generate. Since 4.0's in-app cost isn't public, start your scene in Video 3.0, then move to 4.0 on the shots where duration or keyframe control really changes the result.
