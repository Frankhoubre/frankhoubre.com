## What I mean by AI filmmaking software

Search for "AI movie software" and you get three families mixed together: shot generators (Kling, Firefly, Runway), editing apps with a sprinkle of AI, and tools that promise "a film from one prompt" with avatars. None of them carries a whole film.

A film goes through five stages: script, shot breakdown, storyboard, shot generation, then edit and sound. A generator only covers the fourth. You can absolutely make a short with Kling and an editor, but you'll track everything else by hand, in a spreadsheet or in your head. The day you rewrite a scene, nothing tells you which shots need redoing.

So I kept six tools that cover at least two connected stages, and I ranked them on one main question: does the generated shot stay tied to the text that produced it? After that come the number of stages covered, the video models on offer, and the price of the first tier that allows commercial use.

If you're after the best shot generator instead, I put together [a roundup of AI video tools](/en/blog/meilleurs-outils-ia-video). This page is about the workshop around them.

## 1. ScreenWeaver: when you write your film before you shoot it

Conflict of interest, again: I co-founded this one. I'll stick to what it does today and what it's missing.

ScreenWeaver splits a project into three tabs: Script, Storyboard, Production. You write in a standard screenplay editor (or import a Final Draft, Fountain or WriterDuet file), the app generates the shot breakdown from the text, then you adjust each shot (framing, angle, movement, duration) before generating. Characters, locations and props carry over from shot to shot. In the Production tab every shot becomes a node on a canvas, already wired from the script, where you swap the model or the prompt.

<Screenshot src="screenweaver-pipeline-2026-10-22.webp" alt="The pipeline section of the ScreenWeaver site in French with the three steps Write, Storyboard, Produce and the Storyboard tab of a demo project showing a shot, its characters and the script" caption="The pipeline as the site presents it (French version): shot S3 sits next to its script page and its characters." source="https://www.screenweaver.ai/fr" capturedAt="2026-10-22" />

Seven video models are built in: Seedance 2.5 by default, Seedance 2.0, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, MiniMax H3, Grok Imagine Video 1.5 and Wan 3.0. If you already pay for Higgsfield, a video node can run on your Higgsfield credits for five of those models, plus 10 ScreenWeaver credits per generation.

Lost Garden, our animated series, was made in ScreenWeaver from script to production. Episode 1 runs 17 minutes and took 63 hours of work. Episode 2 runs 21 minutes and took 75 hours. Those are our own published numbers, not an independent test.

There's no editing inside the app. Since October 21, a Trim tool cuts a single video with in and out handles, but you assemble your shots elsewhere. The free Screenwriter plan is writing only, and the paid plans have no trial, just 20 credits when you sign up. Auteur costs $39.99 a month before tax, roughly twice the entry tier of Saga or Higgsfield, and the credit cost of each generation isn't published on the site. Real-time collaboration needs Studio, at $149.99. And video generation has only been open to every subscriber since October 8, 2026.

I rank it first because it's the only one of the six where the text, the breakdown and the generated shot live in the same project, with this many recent video models. If you never write a script, you'll pay for half a tool you don't use, and LTX will suit you better. The head-to-head is in [ScreenWeaver vs LTX Studio](/en/blog/screenweaver-vs-ltx-studio).

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** before you choose, take your hardest scene: two characters, dialogue, an eyeline match. Rewrite one line of it three weeks from now. The right software is the one where you'll still know which shots to redo.

## 2. LTX Studio: when the script exists and you want to finish in one place

LTX Studio, made by Lightricks, sums itself up in three verbs on its homepage: "Write it. Board it. Cut it." In practice you start from an uploaded script, a concept, an image or a video. The tool generates scenes and a storyboard, then hands you an editing timeline and a sound design module. Downstream, it's the most complete of the six: no other tool gets you this close to a finished video without switching apps.

<Screenshot src="ltx-studio-production-2026-10-22.webp" alt="LTX Studio homepage section on production listing Dynamic Storyboard, Timeline Editor and Sound Design, then the four starting points From Script, With a Concept, From an Image and From a Video" caption="LTX Studio's four starting points. From Script reads an existing screenplay. It isn't a place to write one." source="https://ltx.io/studio" capturedAt="2026-10-22" />

Read the price grid closely. The free plan gives 800 credits once, under a personal use license. Lite, at €15 a month (€12 billed yearly), adds upscaling and drops the watermark, still with no commercial use. AI Storyboards, Elements (characters, locations and props you call back by name), Kling 3.0 Pro, Seedance 2.0 and the commercial license arrive with Standard, at €35 a month or €28 yearly, for 28,000 credits. Veo 3.1 needs Pro, at €125 or €100 yearly, which also allows 3 collaborators per project.

## 3. Saga: when writing well matters most and the budget is tight

Saga (CyberFilm AI, createsagas.com) follows the same path as ScreenWeaver, with its strength upstream. Its "How it works" page lays out five steps: plot, characters, scriptwriting, storyboard, polish. Writing is the most polished part, with character archetypes and an act structure on the free plan.

<Screenshot src="saga-etapes-2026-10-22.webp" alt="Saga How it works page showing step 03 Scriptwriting with a standard screenplay editor and step 04 Storyboarding with a grid of panels, each with an Edit button" caption="Saga's steps 3 and 4: a screenplay-format editor, then a panel storyboard you rearrange by hand." source="https://www.createsagas.com/how-it-works" capturedAt="2026-10-22" />

It's the cheapest of the tools that start from a script: Premium at $19.99 a month, with 40 Saga credits. The official FAQ translates them as "up to 40 images or 4 videos per month". Four videos a month will let you preview a scene, and you'll need to buy credits to go further. Extra credits cost $9.99 for 25, and the $149.99 Ultra-Premium plan is still marked "coming soon".

## 4. Autodesk Flow Studio: when you want to place your camera in 3D

Flow Studio (formerly Wonder Studio) starts from a scene. Since August 2026, its 3D Editor lets you assemble a set, animated characters and keyframed cameras, then move into a node-based Canvas that generates images and video from that staging. Autodesk calls it "a shift from prompting to directing".

<Screenshot src="autodesk-flow-studio-3d-editor-2026-10-22.webp" alt="Autodesk blog post introducing the 3D Editor and Canvas in Flow Studio, with the heading One place where ideas come together and an image of a robot character raising a sword on a battlefield" caption="Autodesk pitches the 3D Editor as moving from prompts to staging: set, character and camera get placed before anything is generated." source="https://blogs.autodesk.com/media-and-entertainment/2026/08/04/introducing-3d-editor-canvas-in-autodesk-flow-studio/" capturedAt="2026-10-22" />

No other tool in this ranking gives you that much control over the camera. The rest of Flow Studio comes from VFX: replacing a filmed actor with a 3D character, markerless motion capture, exports to Maya, Blender, Unreal or USD.

The entry pricing is generous. Students aged 18 and over and eligible educators get a free Education plan with 2,100 credits a month. The 3D Editor and Canvas come with every plan.

## 6. Google Flow: when you want to start without paying

Flow is the only tool here you can really use for free with no time limit: 50 credits a day, with or without a subscription, according to the official help center. A Veo 3.1 Lite shot costs 10 credits, a Fast shot 20, a Quality shot 100. That's five Lite shots a day, or two Fast ones, and no Quality shot unless you subscribe to Google AI (200 extra credits a month with Plus, 1,000 with Pro).

To assemble clips, Flow has Scenebuilder. You add clips to a scene, reorder them, trim the start and end, play back the sequence and download it.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-scenebuilder-2026-10-22.webp" alt="Google Flow help page in French, section on building a scene: Scenebuilder arranges several clips in a sequence, reorders them, trims start and end, previews and downloads the scene" caption="Scenebuilder (French help page) assembles clips you've already generated. Only Google models are available, Veo 3.1 and Gemini Omni Flash. The help center describes no script editor and no breakdown. For a whole film, it comes last. To see your first shot tonight without reaching for your card, it's the right pick. If you go that way, learn to [brief Veo like a cinematographer](/en/blog/google-veo-3-brief-realisateur-plan-sequence) and your 50 credits will go further.

## What about editing?

In this ranking, only LTX Studio and Saga have a built-in video editor, and Flow Studio a scene timeline. ScreenWeaver can trim a shot, and that's it. For everything else, you export your shots to a proper editing app.

DaVinci Resolve is the easiest one to recommend: Blackmagic offers [a free version](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/products/davinciresolve) with editing, color and sound in the same app. An AI film needs color work more than most, because two shots from two different models almost never match.

## How to chain the stages without losing the thread

You'll probably end up with two or three tools. The risk at that point: no longer knowing which one holds the current version of your scene. I follow this order whatever the software (the long version is in [my A to Z AI pipeline](/en/blog/pipeline-ia-script-storyboard-production-de-a-z)):

1. Write the scene, even a short one, before generating anything.
2. Break it down shot by shot, with framing, duration and who's on screen. I explain how in [prepping a shot breakdown before you generate](/en/blog/preparer-decoupage-technique-avant-generation-video-ia).
3. Lock your character references before the first shot, or the faces will drift. The method is in [keeping the same character from shot to shot](/en/blog/garder-meme-personnage-video-ia).
4. Generate, then edit, then grade.

If your software covers steps 1 to 3, you have less to glue back together. If it only covers step 4, keep your breakdown in a separate doc and update it with every rewrite. For a budget picture, I priced out [what an AI short film costs](/en/blog/combien-coute-court-metrage-ia-2026) line by line.

## FAQ

### What's the best software for making a film with AI?

Pick by the stage you're at. If you write your own script, ScreenWeaver ties the text, the breakdown and the generated shots together in one project (I co-founded it, as I said up top). If you already have a script and want editing and sound in the same place, LTX Studio goes further downstream, from €35 a month for commercial use. On a small writing budget, Saga starts at $19.99. To stage shots in 3D, Autodesk Flow Studio. No tool does everything perfectly, so start by asking which stage you're tired of doing by hand.

### Is there free AI filmmaking software?

Yes, with limits. Google Flow gives everyone 50 credits a day, which is five Veo 3.1 Lite shots. Autodesk Flow Studio has a free plan with 300 credits a month, and a free Education plan with 2,100 credits for adult students and eligible educators. LTX Studio gives 800 credits once, for personal use. ScreenWeaver and Saga keep writing free, but video generation is paid. For a full film, the free tiers are mostly good for testing your method before you spend money.

### How do you make a film with AI, step by step?

Write the scene first, even if it's one page. Then break it into shots, noting framing, duration and which character is on screen for each. Lock the visual references for your characters before you generate anything, or the faces will change from shot to shot. Generate the shots in breakdown order, cut them together, then color grade to bring the different models closer. Filmmaking software bundles some of these stages. A generator on its own only handles one.

### Which editing software should I use for an AI film?

DaVinci Resolve is the easiest recommendation, because its free version brings editing, color and sound together. Color grading matters more on an AI film than on a regular shoot: two shots made with two different models rarely share the same color science. If you'd rather stay in one tool, LTX Studio has a timeline and sound design, and Saga has a built-in video editor. ScreenWeaver, Higgsfield and Google Flow don't replace an editing app.

### Aren't Kling or Runway filmmaking software?

They're shot generators first. Kling makes videos from text, images or references, and Runway adds tools for reworking existing footage. They're great at the fourth stage of a film, generation, but they don't handle the script or the breakdown. Several tools in this ranking actually include Kling: ScreenWeaver, LTX Studio and Higgsfield all offer it. So you can use Kling inside a pipeline instead of next to one.

### Do I need several subscriptions to make an AI film?

Two is often enough: one tool that holds the script and the breakdown, and an editing app, which is free if you go with DaVinci Resolve. Avoid stacking two tools that do the same job, like two video model aggregators. If you already have Higgsfield, ScreenWeaver can run its video nodes on your Higgsfield credits for five models, plus 10 ScreenWeaver credits per generation. Run the numbers on a real month before you commit to a yearly plan.
