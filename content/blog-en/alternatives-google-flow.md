Started on Google Flow and now going in circles? Pick your next tool based on what's actually blocking you. If you want other engines than Veo (Kling, Seedance) without stacking subscriptions, look at Higgsfield or Runway. If Kling is what you're after, its own plan is still the cheapest way in. If you need a script and a storyboard before any video, go with ScreenWeaver or LTX Studio. And if you're running short tests on no budget, stay on Flow: nobody else gives you 50 credits a day.

I co-founded ScreenWeaver, which is on this list. Every price below was checked on October 31, 2026 on the official pages, from France, and every screenshot was taken that day. I didn't run a single generation for this article. I'm comparing what the companies publish and what users say in public.

## Why people end up looking past Google Flow

Flow is very good at what it was built for, and I went through it in [my Google Flow review](/en/blog/google-flow-avis): generate shots with Veo 3.1 or Gemini Omni, line them up in Scenebuilder, fix a segment by typing what you want changed. All of it with a free way in. The people who leave give reasons you can check yourself in Google's own help center or on its forum.

Credits come first, and [Flow's official help page](https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?hl=en) lays them out. Subscriber or not, everyone gets 50 credits a day. Google AI Plus adds 200 credits a month, Pro adds 1,000, and the two Ultra tiers add 10,000 and 25,000. Monthly credits don't roll over. Without a subscription, the help page also warns that you may not be able to generate video at peak hours, roughly 2 to 5 p.m. UTC.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-credits-2026-10-31.webp" alt="Google Flow help page in French on managing credits: 50 credits a day with or without a subscription, plus 200 a month with Google AI Plus, 1,000 with Pro, and 10,000 or 25,000 with the two Ultra plans" caption="Flow's credit allowance on October 31: 50 a day for everyone, then a monthly top-up depending on your Google AI plan." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-31" />

Then there's the price of a single shot, which swings a lot by model. A Veo 3.1 Lite clip costs 10 credits, Fast costs 20, Quality costs 100. The help page spells out that this is per generation, not per request, so a request that returns two videos is billed twice. Your free day buys five Lite shots or two Fast ones. One Quality shot costs more than your whole daily allowance.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-cout-generation-2026-10-31.webp" alt="Google Flow help table of credits per generation: Veo 3.1 Lite 10 credits, Fast 20, Quality 100 for an 8 second video, Gemini Omni Flash 720p from 7 to 15 credits depending on length" caption="What one shot costs in Flow. A dialogue scene in Veo 3.1 Quality burns through a Pro plan in a few days." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16526234?hl=fr" capturedAt="2026-10-31" />

The model list is the next complaint. Flow only runs Google models: three versions of Veo 3.1, Gemini Omni Flash, and Nano Banana for images. No Kling, no Seedance, no MiniMax. If an action shot needs Kling's motion, or you want Seedance's reference images to hold a face, you have to leave Flow.

Europe is its own problem. The official help page lists two features that don't work in the European Economic Area, Switzerland or the UK: editing videos you upload, and avatars. Working from Paris or Berlin, you can't use Omni to rework footage shot somewhere else, and that's one of Flow's selling points.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-restrictions-europe-2026-10-31.webp" alt="Where you can use Google Flow help page with sections expanded: editing uploaded videos and avatars are not supported in the European Economic Area, Switzerland and the United Kingdom" caption="The two Flow features blocked in Europe, according to Google's English help page." source="https://support.google.com/flow/answer/16353544?hl=en" capturedAt="2026-10-31" />

Continuity is where it hurts most. On [Google's developer forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/veo-flow-generation-issues-lost-credits-consistency-problems-and-excessive-failed-generations/147374), a thread opened on May 22, 2026 by someone who publishes episodic stories has been read more than 2,000 times by 19 participants. It lists what a lot of people run into: refusals on ordinary scenes (walking, sitting at a table), shoes and hairstyles that change between two clips despite references, and the feeling that every clip starts from zero again. That's one person's account, not a measurement, but it lines up with other threads on the same forum about lost credits.

<Screenshot src="google-flow-forum-continuite-2026-10-31.webp" alt="Post in the Veo Flow Generation Issues thread on the Build with Google AI forum: the section Character consistency is extremely unstable lists outfits, shoes, hairstyles and accessories changing from one clip to the next" caption="The most detailed complaint in the thread: recurring characters don't stay the same from one clip to the next." source="https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/veo-flow-generation-issues-lost-credits-consistency-problems-and-excessive-failed-generations/147374" capturedAt="2026-10-31" />

And Flow has no idea what a script is. The help center covers generation, Scenebuilder and the agent, with no screenplay editor and no shot list. You write one prompt per shot. On a music video you can live with that. On a 12 minute short you'll rewrite three times, you lose track fast.

## What Flow really costs

Before comparing, know what you're paying now. Flow doesn't have its own plan. It comes bundled with Google AI plans. On October 31, viewed from France, Google One listed Plus at €4.99 a month, Pro at €21.99, Ultra at €99.99 and the top Ultra tier at €219.99.

<Screenshot src="google-ai-forfaits-france-2026-10-31.webp" alt="Google One comparison table for France: Google AI Plus 4.99 euros a month, Pro 21.99 euros, Ultra 5x 99.99 euros, Ultra 20x 219.99 euros, with 200, 1,000, 10,000 and 25,000 Google Flow credits a month" caption="Google AI plans as seen from France on October 31. Flow is included, with a credit allowance that climbs by tier." source="https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/" capturedAt="2026-10-31" />

Run the numbers on one minute of film. That's eight 8 second shots if you keep every take. Nobody keeps every take, so say three attempts per kept shot, which is on the optimistic side for fiction (in my Flow review I counted four). In Veo 3.1 Fast that's 24 generations at 20 credits, or 480 credits a minute. In Quality it's 2,400 credits, more than twice what a Pro subscriber gets in a month. On Pro, counting the 50 daily credits, you can cut roughly two minutes of film a month in Fast. Great for learning. For a full episode you need Ultra at €99.99.

> 💡 **Frank's cut:** count your takes before you hit generate. On Lost Garden, the bill grows on the retries to hold a face or a gesture, not on the first render. Whatever the tool, budget three times what your shot list says.

## Higgsfield: every engine in one place

If Flow's model list is what frustrates you, Higgsfield is an aggregator: Seedance 2.5 and 2.0, Kling 3.0 and its variants, Veo 3.1 and Veo 3.1 Fast, Wan, plus in-house tools like Cinema Studio and Popcorn for storyboards. You can shoot an action beat in Kling and the reverse angle in Seedance without switching subscriptions.

<Screenshot src="higgsfield-tarifs-2026-10-31.webp" alt="Higgsfield pricing page in euros, billed yearly: Starter 19 euros with 270 credits and access to Seedance 2.0 Fast and Mini, Plus 47 euros instead of 59 with 1,200 credits, Ultra 99 euros instead of 129 with 3,000 credits" caption="Higgsfield's plans on October 31, billed yearly and before VAT. Starter only unlocks a selection of models." source="https://higgsfield.ai/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-31" />

Watch the entry tier. Starter, at €19 a month before VAT, gives you 270 credits and only Seedance 2.0 Fast and 2.0 Mini, with the line "Access to selected models only." Every model comes with Plus: €59 a month, or €47 billed yearly, for 1,200 credits. The rate card lists about 40 credits for 4 seconds of Veo 3.1 at 1080p and about 8 credits for 5 seconds of Kling 3.0 at 1080p. Veo costs more than Kling here, same as everywhere else.

The "unlimited" in the banner is temporary (7 days on some models, depending on the plan) and only works on higgsfield.ai, not through MCP or Canvas. It's written at the bottom of the pricing page. Higgsfield is still a shot tool: no script, and Popcorn holds consistency across 8 images at most. I covered what it does well and where it stops in [my Higgsfield review](/en/blog/higgsfield-avis-realisateur), and what to use instead for a full film in [the Higgsfield alternatives](/en/blog/alternatives-higgsfield-film).

If you already break your scenes down and want to compare engines shot by shot, this is the most natural step up from Flow.

## Runway: editing as much as generating

Runway comes at it from another angle. Its in-house model, Gen-4.5, sits next to Kling 4.0, Seedance 2.5 and Nano Banana Pro in the paid plans. And the tool was built around editing: Aleph 2.0 to change an existing shot, Workflows to chain steps together.

Monthly: $15, $35 and $95." source="https://runwayml.com/pricing" capturedAt="2026-10-31" />

According to [Runway's pricing page](https://runwayml.com/pricing), the free plan gives 125 credits once, enough to look around. Standard costs $15 a month ($12 billed yearly) for 625 credits, which Runway itself translates into 52 seconds of Gen-4.5. Pro goes to $35 ($28 yearly) for 2,250 credits, or 187 seconds of Gen-4.5. On October 31 the page also advertised 25% off Kling 4.0 generations until November 30.

Next to Flow you pay more per second, but you pick your engine and you can rework your footage. I didn't find Veo on Runway's pricing page that day, so if Veo suits you and only the price bothers you, Runway won't fix that. ## Kling AI: the cheapest way to get one engine

If leaving Flow mostly means wanting Kling, get it at the source. It's the lowest price on this list: Standard at $6.99 for the first month, then $8.80 a month, for 660 monthly credits. The page says generated content can be used commercially from Standard up, with 1080p and 4K. Pro is $25.99 for the first month, then $32.56, for 3,000 credits.

## LTX Studio: a storyboard before the video

LTX Studio partly solves the script problem: you import your screenplay, it builds a storyboard, then you generate and edit in the same space. It has its own model, LTX-2.5, on every plan, and adds Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0 and Google's image models from Standard up.

## When to stay on Google Flow

Stay on Flow if you're learning, if you're testing an idea in a handful of shots, or if your project fits in 8 to 10 second clips you'll edit somewhere else. No alternative gives you 50 credits a day without a card, and Google AI Plus at €4.99 is still unbeatable to get started. Gemini Omni has a real edge too: you rework a 10 second segment by typing what you want changed. In Europe, that only works on videos generated inside Flow, not on footage you upload.

Leave if you need another engine than Veo, if your characters have to stay the same over thirty shots, if you're writing a script you'll keep rewriting, or if you spend your evenings waiting for peak hours to end. And plan your shot list before you switch: [preparing a shot list before you generate](/en/blog/preparer-decoupage-technique-avant-generation-video-ia) will save you from redoing the same failed shots in a new tool.

## FAQ

### What is the best alternative to Google Flow?

Start from what's blocking you in Flow. If you want engines other than Veo, Higgsfield puts Seedance, Kling, Veo and Wan on one plan, from €59 a month before VAT for every model. If you want to rework existing shots, Runway and its Aleph tool. If you only want Kling, its own plan at $8.80 a month. If you write scripts, ScreenWeaver or LTX Studio. I co-founded ScreenWeaver, so keep that in mind as you read this ranking.

### Is there a free alternative to Google Flow?

Not at the same level. Flow gives everyone 50 credits a day, which is five Veo 3.1 Lite shots daily, and no competitor offers a refreshing allowance that generous. Runway gives 125 credits once, LTX Studio 800 credits once, Higgsfield limited use. ScreenWeaver has a free-forever plan, but it covers writing the script, not video. To test without paying, Flow is still the best starting point. You move on once you want to produce.

### Can I use Veo 3.1 outside Google Flow?

Yes. Higgsfield lists Veo 3.1 and Veo 3.1 Fast, at about 40 credits for 4 seconds at 1080p according to its pricing page on October 31, 2026. LTX Studio keeps it for its Pro plan, at €125 a month. ScreenWeaver includes it from the Auteur plan, at $39.99 a month before tax, next to Seedance, Kling and MiniMax. Runway didn't show it on its pricing page that day. Keeping Veo while adding other engines is often the best compromise.

### Why do my generations keep failing in Google Flow?

Public reports point to two main causes. First, safety refusals on ordinary scenes, with a "can't generate that video" message and no explanation, laid out in a Google forum thread opened in May 2026. Second, congestion: without a subscription, the official help page warns that video may be unavailable between 2 and 5 p.m. UTC. Rephrase the prompt with a simpler description of the action, leave out public figures (blocked in every region), and move your generations outside peak hours.

### Is Google Flow limited in Europe?

Flow is available across Europe for anyone over 18, but two features are blocked. According to the official help page, editing uploaded videos and avatars aren't supported in the European Economic Area, Switzerland or the United Kingdom. You can generate, edit in Scenebuilder and rework videos made in Flow, but you can't use Omni on footage shot elsewhere. From France, Google AI plans showed €4.99, €21.99, €99.99 and €219.99 a month on October 31, 2026.

### Which Google Flow alternative keeps the same character?

No tool fixes character drift on its own, but some help more than Flow. Seedance 2.5 accepts lots of reference images and is the default model in ScreenWeaver; it's also on Higgsfield and Runway. LTX Studio offers Elements you call up by name, from the Standard plan. Higgsfield's Popcorn holds consistency across 8 images. Either way, a character sheet built before the first shot matters more than which engine you pick.

### How much does a minute of film cost in Google Flow?

Using the official rate card from October 31, 2026, a minute in Veo 3.1 Fast is eight 8 second shots at 20 credits each. Counting three attempts per kept shot, that comes to 480 credits a minute, and 2,400 credits in Veo 3.1 Quality. A Pro plan at €21.99 gives 1,000 credits a month plus 50 a day, or roughly two minutes of film in Fast. For a full episode, aim for Ultra at €99.99 and its 10,000 credits.
